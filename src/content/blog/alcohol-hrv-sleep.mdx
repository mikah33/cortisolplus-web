## Does alcohol lower HRV?

**In wearable studies, drinking more alcohol was linked to lower overnight heart rate variability (HRV) and a higher resting heart rate.**

HRV is the small change in time between your heartbeats.

If you see this pattern after drinking, it can be useful to know. But your watch can't tell you what caused a single change. It also can't measure your cortisol (your body's main stress hormone).

Cortisol+ publishes this guide. We didn't run these studies, and what they found doesn't prove our app's score is right.

## What did the research find?

### A big 2026 study

A [2026 study in PLOS Digital Health](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0001284) looked at 5,109,185 person-days from 20,968 people.

Drinking more alcohol was linked to:

- a higher resting heart rate at night
- lower HRV
- shorter sleep

The researchers looked at changes within each person. They also adjusted for several things, including how often people drank and the season.

### What that study can't tell you

This study watched what happened in wearable data. It wasn't an experiment, so it can't prove the cause of every change.

It used WHOOP data. WHOOP supported it, and some of the authors worked for the company.

Don't turn its findings into an exact prediction for an Apple Watch user, or for a Cortisol+ score.

### An earlier study

An [earlier study of 4,098 Finnish employees](https://mental.jmir.org/2018/1/e23) also looked at drinking and the body's automatic nervous system in the first hours of sleep. Like the first one, it observed people rather than testing them.

Together, these studies suggest alcohol is worth noting as part of the story around a reading. They don't mean you should diagnose yourself from a graph.

## How much will my HRV drop after drinking?

**There's no fixed number we can promise.**

People are different. So are the amounts they drink, when they drink, and how each device measures. A research average is not a forecast for you.

An app score also isn't in the same unit as HRV, which is measured in milliseconds (ms).

For example, a ten-point change in a wellness score is **not** a ten-millisecond change in HRV. Check the measure and the unit on the screen you're reading.

## How do I check my Apple Watch data?

Use nights from your normal routine. **There's no need to drink just to test it.**

1. Find the original HRV records in Apple Health. Check their dates, times and source.
2. Look at sleep and resting heart rate for the same days. Check that each summary covers the same hours.
3. Note when you drank, if you did. Also note unusual exercise, travel, illness or broken sleep.
4. Compare several similar nights, using the same device and the same way of measuring.
5. Keep your conclusion small. "These changed together" is not the same as "This proves what caused it."

### Watch out for different kinds of HRV

Apple explains its method, called SDNN, in its [heart measurement technical paper](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/Heart_Rate_Calorimetry_Activity_on_Apple_Watch_November_2024.pdf).

Other wearables often use a different method, called RMSSD. Don't compare the two numbers as if they were the same.

For a fuller checklist, read [low HRV during sleep on Apple Watch](/blog/hrv-during-sleep/). If your HRV record is missing, start with [HRV troubleshooting](/blog/apple-watch-hrv-not-updating/).

## If my HRV looks normal, was drinking harmless?

**No.** A wearable only sums up a few signals.

A reading close to your usual range doesn't show that alcohol had no effects. It also doesn't show that drinking is safe for you.

The [National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/alcohols-effects-health/alcohols-effects-body) describes effects that go well beyond what a watch measures.

## Does low HRV show a cortisol spike?

**No.** A lower HRV number can't confirm high cortisol.

[Cortisol tests](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cortisol-test/) measure the hormone in blood, saliva or urine. Our [Apple Watch cortisol guide](/stress/apple-watch/) explains the difference between sensor readings, wellness scores and hormone tests.

## How should I use this data?

**Use it as background, not as a verdict.**

The [free stress and sleep journal](/resources/stress-sleep-journal/) lets you write down the date, your sleep times, the readings you have and your own notes. It doesn't give you a hormone level or a medical risk score.

If changes keep happening, or you have worrying symptoms, talk to a healthcare professional.
