**A sore jaw, or someone telling you that you grind your teeth at night, is a reason to think about a dental check.**

It's not proof that your cortisol stayed high overnight.

## What is bruxism?

**Bruxism means clenching or grinding your teeth.** It can happen while you're awake or asleep.

The [National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/bruxism) describes several things that can play a part, including stress. It doesn't point to one single hormone.

It can lead to:

- Damaged teeth
- Sensitive teeth
- Jaw discomfort

  Before your dental visit, <strong>jot down a few notes</strong> on when your jaw feels sore. Mornings? After work? It helps your dentist.
</ZenTip>

## What should I tell my dentist?

**Bring a few simple notes.** Write down whether you notice:

- Clenching during the day
- Soreness in the morning
- Sensitive teeth
- Headaches

If someone has heard you grinding while you sleep, tell your dentist. Mention any medicines and any sleep worries.

The dentist can check your teeth and jaw. They can decide whether more tests would help.

**The right care depends on that check.** A mouth guard may protect your teeth. Other options may help with habits or related problems.

Stress management can be part of care. But a promise to "fix the cortisol root cause" skips the check you need. The options are described in the [NIDCR treatment guide](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/bruxism#treatment).

## How do I use watch data carefully?

**Keep your notes and your watch data separate.**

You might choose to note your sleep or daily stress next to clenching episodes. But:

- A poor sleep estimate doesn't show that grinding happened.
- An HRV (heart rate variability) change doesn't tell you the cause.
