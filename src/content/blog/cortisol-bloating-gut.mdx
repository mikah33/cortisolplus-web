You've been stressed for weeks. Suddenly your jeans feel tight. Your stomach is puffy and uncomfortable, but you haven't changed what you eat.

This might be cortisol bloating. It's when long-term stress shows up in your gut.

It's more than "feeling bloated when stressed." It's a pattern where ongoing stress upsets your digestion. That may lead to slower digestion, gas and a swollen-looking belly.

Knowing the link helps you spot it and deal with the cause.

## How does cortisol affect digestion?

**When cortisol rises, your body moves into survival mode.** Cortisol is your body's main stress hormone ([StatPearls: Physiology, Cortisol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538239/)).

Blood flow moves away from digestion toward your muscles and brain. That helps if you're running from danger. It causes problems when stress goes on and on.

Long-term stress can slow parts of your gut, so food may sit longer before it moves on.

The result can be that "stuck" feeling, where food seems to just sit in your stomach.

Stress can also change the balance of bacteria in your gut. Research shows that short-term and long-term stress change how your gut moves, what it releases, how leaky it is, and how inflamed it gets. This happens through the gut-brain axis, the two-way link between your gut and brain ([Leigh et al., 2023, *The Journal of Physiology*](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1113/JP281951)).

That can mean more gas. It can also make you more sensitive to normal amounts of gas that wouldn't usually bother you.

## Why does cortisol make me hold water?

**Cortisol can also affect how your body handles salt and water.**

At very high levels, cortisol can act like aldosterone. That's another hormone that tells your kidneys to hold on to sodium (salt).

More sodium means you hold more water. That can show up as:

- Puffiness around your middle
- Swelling in your hands and feet
- Feeling "inflated" in a way that's different from fat gain
- Weight that goes up and down from day to day

This kind of bloating may feel firm, not soft. It may get worse as the day goes on and ease a little after a good night's sleep.

## What is the stress, gut and inflammation loop?

**Long-term stress may trap you in a loop.**

Long-term stress can weaken your gut barrier. That's the tight layer of cells that keeps undigested food bits inside your gut, where they belong ([Leigh et al., 2023, *The Journal of Physiology*](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1113/JP281951)). When this barrier gets "leaky," small bits can cross into your blood.

Here's how the loop goes:

1. Your immune system sees these bits as invaders and causes inflammation.
2. The inflammation can cause more bloating and discomfort.
3. If the stress keeps going, the cycle can keep going too.

Some people notice they react to foods they used to eat just fine during stressful times.

## What does cortisol bloating feel like?

**It has a few signs that set it apart** from other gut problems:

- It shows up or gets worse during stressful times, not at random.
- Your stomach may look swollen, especially the upper belly.
- The bloating doesn't always match what or how much you ate.
- You may feel full fast, but hungry again soon after.
- It gets better on holidays or during calm stretches.

If you have several [cortisol symptoms](/cortisol/symptoms/) along with gut changes, stress may be playing a part. A doctor can help rule out other causes.

## How can I ease stress bloating?

**Aim at the stress itself, not just the bloating.** Quick fixes like anti-gas medicines might help for a while. They won't fix the cause.

### Start with stress basics

- **Keep regular sleep times.** This helps your body keep a steady daily rhythm.
- **Move, but don't overtrain.** Exercise helps, but too much hard exercise can keep cortisol high.
- **Eat regular meals.** This can help keep your energy steady.

### Make digestion easier on stressful days

- Choose cooked vegetables instead of raw.
- Eat smaller meals more often instead of big ones.
- Drink enough water, but don't gulp large amounts at once. That can make bloating worse.

Some people find that eating less salt during stressful times limits water retention. Others do well with gentle movement, like walking after meals, to help digestion when stress has slowed it down.
