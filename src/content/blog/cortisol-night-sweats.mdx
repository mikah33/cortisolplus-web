**Waking up sweaty at 2, 3 or 4 a.m. doesn't point to a cortisol problem.**

The time of an episode can't tell a hormone condition apart from other causes.

## Is it a warm bed or real night sweats?

**Night sweats soak your clothes and bedding, even in a cool room.** That's how the [NHS describes night sweats](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/night-sweats/).

Possible causes include:

- Menopause
- Anxiety
- Medicines
- Other conditions

Sometimes no cause is found.

### When should I get help?

**If sweats happen regularly, wake you up or worry you, get medical advice.**

The NHS also advises getting checked if sweats come with unexplained weight loss, or symptoms such as a high temperature, cough or diarrhea.

<ZenTip image="/app-ui/zen_new_tour_sleep.webp">
  Keep a pen and paper <strong>by your bed</strong>. A quick note in the night is easier than trying to remember in the morning.
</ZenTip>

## What should I write down?

**Record what happened, without guessing the cause.** Note:

- Whether you needed to change your clothes or bedding
- Whether the room was hot
- Any other symptoms

Add the names of any medicines or supplements you take to what you bring to the appointment.

That gives your clinician a better starting point than an assumed "cortisol spike."

**A sleep journal can help keep track of dates.** But don't wait to finish a week of tracking before getting care for symptoms that worry you. Use your notes as a helper, not a gatekeeper.

## What can't the Watch tell me?

**A Watch sleep record may help you remember a broken night.** That's about it.

- Wrist temperature and HRV (heart rate variability) are not cortisol measurements.
- They don't diagnose why you're sweating.
- A lower Cortisol+ score the next day doesn't mean the cause has gone away.
