When you're stressed, do you reach for cookies, candy or a sugary coffee drink? You're not imagining the link.

**Cortisol sugar cravings are real.** They're linked with changes in your body when stress hormones stay high.

This isn't about willpower. When cortisol rises, your body switches into a mode that makes sweet foods extra tempting. Knowing why gives you better ways to break the cycle.

## How does cortisol cause sugar cravings?

**One of cortisol's main jobs is to raise your blood sugar.**

Cortisol is your body's main stress hormone. When you face a threat, real or imagined, your adrenal glands release cortisol to help you respond. Raising blood sugar gives your muscles and brain fuel.

When stress goes on and on, cortisol can keep your blood sugar (glucose) higher than usual.

High cortisol may also affect your brain:

- It may change hunger and fullness signals.
- It seems to boost the "reward" feeling when you eat sweet or high-fat foods. So sugar feels more satisfying when you're stressed, and the habit gets stronger.

**Put together, you may crave sugar more and feel less satisfied after normal amounts.**

## Why does my brain want fast carbs when I'm stressed?

**Your brain is hungry, and stress tells it to grab the fastest fuel.**

Your brain uses about 20% of your body's energy, even though it's only about 2% of your body weight. Under stress, your body leans toward fast fuel.

Simple sugars get into your blood quickly. They don't need much digestion. Long ago, that made sense: if you were running from danger, you needed quick energy, not a slow meal.

Today, most of our stress is long-term and mental, not a physical emergency. But your body can't tell a work deadline from real danger. It can react in a similar way: more cortisol and cravings for the fastest fuel around.

That's why stress eating usually goes for:

- Candy and chocolate
- Baked goods and pastries
- Sugary drinks and energy drinks
- White bread and processed snacks
- Ice cream and desserts

These foods raise blood sugar fast and bring short relief. **But they can also keep the cycle going.**

## What is the stress-craving loop?

**Once it starts, the pattern can feed itself.** It often goes like this:

1. Long-term stress keeps cortisol up.
2. Sweet, quick-energy foods feel more tempting.
3. You eat sugar for quick relief.
4. You get a quick lift, then often a dip in energy.
5. Feeling tired and cranky adds to your stress.
6. The cycle repeats.

The more it happens, the more your brain learns that sugar is the answer to stress.

**To break the loop, you need to step in at more than one point**, not just try to resist cravings with willpower.

## How do I break the cortisol-sugar cycle?

**The goal isn't perfection.** It's having fewer and smaller blood sugar swings, because those can feed cravings.

- **Keep your blood sugar steady.** Eat protein and fiber with every meal. They slow digestion and help prevent big spikes and crashes. Even adding protein to a carb-heavy snack makes a difference.
- **Choose your carbs wisely.** You don't need to cut out carbs. But picking complex carbs (whole grains, vegetables, beans and lentils) over simple sugars keeps blood sugar steadier. Have sweeter foods after a meal, when the other food slows how fast the sugar is absorbed.
- **Work on the stress itself.** Managing blood sugar helps. But if stress stays high, cravings may keep coming. Look at your sleep, work stress, relationship tension, or whatever keeps your stress response switched on. Even small changes, like a 10-minute walk or five minutes of breathing exercises, can help you feel calmer.
- **Don't skip meals.** It's easy to skip meals when you're busy and stressed. But it can leave you very hungry later, which makes cravings worse. Eating regularly keeps your energy steadier.
- **Look at foods that help lower cortisol.** Some foods may help as part of a balanced diet. Learn more in our guide on [foods that lower cortisol](/cortisol/lower/foods/).
- **Get enough sleep.** Poor sleep can raise cortisol and make blood sugar harder to control. It can also make high-calorie foods more tempting.
