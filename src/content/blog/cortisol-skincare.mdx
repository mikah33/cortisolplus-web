**Yes, stress and cortisol can play a part in how your skin looks and feels.**

The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says the hormones released when you're stressed, like cortisol, affect the way your body works. Feeling stressed can affect several skin and hair conditions ([AAD: 6 skin and hair conditions linked to stress](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).

It works both ways, too. Living with a skin condition can make you feel even more stressed.

The good news: a simple, gentle routine plus a calmer week can help your skin on both fronts.

## How can stress affect my skin?

**Stress can make skin more easily irritated, inflamed and itchy.**

Your brain and skin talk to each other. With chronic (long-term) stress, the skin "is both a target and a source of stress hormones." That can make it more prone to itch, inflammation, irritation and infection ([AAD news release](https://www.aad.org/news/stress-shows-in-skin-hair-nails)).

Here's how that can show up:

- **Acne.** Stress can make acne worse. When you're stressed, your body releases hormones that make your skin produce more oil. That oil can clog pores and lead to a flare-up ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)). There's more on this in our guide to [cortisol acne](/blog/cortisol-acne-breakouts/).
- **Eczema.** Stress doesn't cause eczema, but it can make it feel itchier. Because stress makes it harder for skin to heal, it can also make eczema flares last longer ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).
- **Psoriasis.** Stress doesn't cause psoriasis. But some people with psoriasis say stress can trigger their flare-ups ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).
- **Hives.** Some people get hives (itchy, raised welts) when they're very stressed ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions); [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11874-stress)).
- **Slower healing.** Stress can slow down wound healing ([AAD news release](https://www.aad.org/news/stress-shows-in-skin-hair-nails)).

If you already have a skin condition, chronic stress can make it worse ([MedlinePlus: Stress and your health](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003211.htm)).

## What does a stress-aware skincare routine look like?

**Keep it short, gentle and easy to do when you're tired.** A stressful week is not the time for a 10-step routine.

### Wash gently

The AAD's face-washing tips ([AAD: Face washing 101](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/face-washing-101)):

- **Use a gentle, non-abrasive cleanser** that doesn't contain alcohol.
- **Use your fingertips** and lukewarm water. Washcloths and sponges can irritate skin.
- **Don't scrub.** Scrubbing irritates the skin.
- **Pat dry** with a soft towel.
- **Wash twice a day**, and after heavy sweating.

### Moisturise

- **Moisturise if your skin is dry or itchy** ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/care/face-washing-101)).
- **Apply it while your skin is still damp**, after a shower or washing ([AAD: Tips to relieve dry skin](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).
- **Pick fragrance-free products.** Dermatologists recommend them for dry, sensitive skin. "Unscented" isn't the same thing ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).
- **Creams and ointments** add more moisture than lotions ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).
- **Keep showers short**, about 5 to 10 minutes, in warm rather than hot water ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).

### Protect

- **Use a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher.** Seek shade and wear sun-protective clothing too ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).

### Look after the stress side

Skincare is only half of it. **If stress affects your skin, you also need to manage your stress** ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).

- **Sleep.** Getting enough sleep, eating well and moving your body can help your body handle stress better ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11874-stress)).
- **Mind-body practices.** Meditation, yoga, visualisation and breathing exercises can help reduce stress, alongside treatment for your skin ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).
- **More support.** A therapist, a support group or medicine for stress are options if you need more help ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).

## What can skincare products do, and what can't they do?

**Products can clean, soothe, moisturise and protect your skin. They can't lower your cortisol.**

In the US, the FDA defines cosmetics as products used for cleansing, beautifying or changing how you look. Products meant to treat disease or change how the body works are regulated as drugs. Most cosmetics don't need FDA approval before they go on sale ([FDA: Is it a cosmetic, a drug, or both?](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap)).

So be wary of creams sold as "cortisol-lowering" or "stress-hormone balancing." A cream can't fix the stress behind a flare.

**Keep it simple:**

- **More isn't better.** Many anti-itch products contain ingredients that can irritate very dry skin and make it worse. Ask a dermatologist first ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).
- **Stop anything that burns, stings or irritates**, and tell your dermatologist ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).
- **Skin conditions still need medical treatment.** Stress tools help, but they don't replace treatment ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/stress-skin-conditions)).

Worried about thin or easily bruised skin? Steroid creams, age and sun can all play a part. Read [cortisol and skin thinning](/blog/cortisol-skin-thinning/), and talk to your clinician before changing any prescribed treatment.

## When should I see a GP or dermatologist?

**If stress is affecting your skin, hair or nails, a board-certified dermatologist can help** ([AAD news release](https://www.aad.org/news/stress-shows-in-skin-hair-nails)).

### Acne

See a GP if ([NHS: Acne](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/acne/)):

- You've tried treating it yourself and it isn't getting better
- You have lots of spots, or they're deep, painful or scarring
- You have signs of a skin infection, like hot, swollen, red skin
- It's affecting your mental health

### Eczema

See a GP if treatments aren't helping ([NHS: Atopic eczema](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/atopic-eczema/)).

**Get an urgent GP appointment or call NHS 111** if your eczema ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/atopic-eczema/)):

- Is blistered, crusty, leaking fluid or has pus-filled spots
- Is painful, swollen or feels warm
- Suddenly gets worse or spreads
- Comes with a high temperature, or you feel generally unwell

These can be signs of an infection.

### Dry skin and anything new

- **Dry skin that doesn't improve** after trying dermatologists' tips can be a sign of an underlying condition. See a dermatologist ([AAD](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/dry/dermatologists-tips-relieve-dry-skin)).
- **New or unusual symptoms**, or stress that feels overwhelming, are a reason to contact your doctor ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003211.htm)).
