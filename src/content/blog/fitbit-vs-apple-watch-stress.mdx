If you're choosing a wearable, the **Fitbit vs Apple Watch stress** question matters. Both say they track stress. But they measure different things, and both have real limits.

Here's what each one does, what the science says, and how to pick.

## How does each watch measure stress?

**Neither one measures cortisol or other stress hormones.** Both use heart rate variability (HRV), the change in time between heartbeats, plus other body data to estimate your stress.

**The key difference:** Fitbit packs everything into one daily rating. Apple Watch gives you raw data that apps can make sense of.

## What does HRV tell you about stress?

HRV measures the time gaps between heartbeats.

- **Higher HRV** usually means your nervous system is balanced.
- **Lower HRV** often signals physical stress, poor recovery or burnout.

But HRV has limits. It shows what your nervous system is doing *right now*. That isn't always the same as how stressed you feel. You might have high HRV while worrying about work, or low HRV after a hard workout while you feel mentally relaxed.

**HRV is most useful for spotting stress *over time*, not minute-to-minute feelings.** Ongoing stress is generally linked with lower HRV. Short bursts of mental stress can change HRV in different ways depending on the person and the situation.

Both [Fitbit](/fitbit/) and Apple Watch track [HRV](/hrv/) during sleep. That's actually more reliable than daytime readings, because fewer things get in the way, like movement, caffeine or digestion.

## What is Fitbit's EDA Scan?

Fitbit's electrodermal activity (EDA) sensor measures tiny sweat gland activity on your skin, which changes when your fight-or-flight system switches on.

**How it works:** you hold the device still with your hand or fingers during an "EDA Scan". The watch counts your EDA responses. More responses generally mean your body is more stirred up.

**The upside:** EDA is a well-known tool in stress research. It can catch fight-or-flight activity that HRV might miss.

**The downside:** you have to remember to do the scan yourself. Many people do it once or twice and then forget. It also measures how stirred up you are, not stress itself. Excitement and anxiety can look the same.

## How accurate are they?

**Independent research on these stress features is limited.**

What we know:

- Wrist HRV readings are most reliable when you're still, such as during sleep.
- Accuracy drops when you move or exercise.
- Fitbit's Resilience rating mixes more than ten factors. That can smooth out noise, but it makes it harder to see what's driving your rating.
- Apple Watch HRV data can be shared with other apps through Apple Health, so you can see the raw numbers.

**The bigger issue:** even perfect HRV measurement isn't perfect stress tracking. HRV shows what your body is doing, and that's only part of stress.

## Which one should you choose?

**Both fall short if:**

- you need real cortisol levels (neither measures this)
- you want reliable stress alerts in the moment (Fitbit's Body Responses on Sense and Pixel Watch 2 and later flag sudden spikes, but exercise, heat or caffeine can trigger them too)
- you expect medical-grade accuracy

**The honest answer:** for basic stress awareness, both work reasonably well. For serious stress management, you need more than a wearable score.
