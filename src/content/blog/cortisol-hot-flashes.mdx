A hot flash during a stressful moment does not prove that cortisol caused it. A wearable stress score cannot separate menopause-related symptoms from other reasons for feeling hot or sweating.

## Menopause provides context, not a self-diagnosis

Hot flashes, night sweats and changes in sleep can occur during the menopause transition. Symptoms and their impact vary. [The National Institute on Aging explains the range of menopause experiences](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/menopause/what-menopause).

It is useful to describe what happened without assigning a hormone explanation: how the episode felt, whether it interrupted sleep, and whether it is becoming more frequent. A pattern in a diary can support a conversation; it cannot confirm which hormone caused that pattern.

## What to record

Make a brief note of the time, duration, room conditions and effect on your activities or sleep. If a medicine or supplement changed recently, include its name in the notes you bring to your clinician. Keep sensitive details in a place you control.

If symptoms are new, concerning or interfering with life, discuss them with a healthcare professional. [NIA's hot-flash guide describes options to discuss](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/hot-flashes-what-can-i-do). The appropriate choice depends on your history and preferences; a watch reading does not select a treatment.

## Can Cortisol+ confirm the connection?

No. Cortisol+ shows available HRV, sleep and related wearable inputs. Those records do not identify the cause of a hot flash or demonstrate that a breathing exercise changed cortisol.
