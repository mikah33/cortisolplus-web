**A hot flash during a stressful moment doesn't prove that cortisol caused it.**

A watch stress score also can't tell menopause symptoms apart from other reasons you might feel hot or sweaty.

## Is it menopause?

**Menopause gives useful context, but it's not something to diagnose yourself.**

Hot flashes, night sweats and sleep changes can happen during the move into menopause. Symptoms, and how much they affect you, are different for everyone. [The National Institute on Aging explains the range of menopause experiences](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/menopause/what-menopause).

It helps to describe what happened, without naming a hormone as the cause:

- How did the episode feel?
- Did it wake you or break up your sleep?
- Is it happening more often?

A pattern in a diary can help a conversation with your doctor. It can't confirm which hormone caused it.

<ZenTip image="/app-ui/zen/zen-journal.webp">
  Keep notes <strong>short and simple</strong>: the time, how long it lasted, and how warm the room was. That's enough to be useful.
</ZenTip>

## What should I write down?

**A short note for each episode is enough.** Include:

- The time
- How long it lasted
- How warm the room was
- How it affected what you were doing, or your sleep

If you recently started or changed a medicine or supplement, add its name to the notes you bring to your clinician. Keep private details somewhere you control.

**If symptoms are new, worrying or getting in the way of life, talk to a healthcare professional.** [NIA's hot-flash guide describes options to discuss](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/hot-flashes-what-can-i-do).

The right choice depends on your history and what you prefer. A watch reading doesn't pick a treatment.
