A change in sex drive does not establish high cortisol or low testosterone. It can matter to you without having a simple hormone explanation.

## Consider the whole situation

The [NHS guide to low libido](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/loss-of-libido/) describes possible contributors including stress, relationships, sexual discomfort, pregnancy, menopause, medicines and health conditions. Several can overlap.

That makes the idea that cortisol “steals” the material needed for sex hormones an unhelpful shortcut for an individual symptom. Neither your experience nor an app score demonstrates that mechanism in your body.

## Prepare a conversation you are comfortable having

If the change worries you, discuss it with a healthcare professional. You can describe when it began, whether it is a change from your usual experience, whether there is pain or another sexual concern, and whether medicines changed around the same time.

You do not need to provide a wearable chart to justify the appointment. If discussing the topic feels difficult, a short written note can help you state what you want help with. Treatment depends on the cause; discuss suspected medication effects with the prescriber before changing treatment.

## Where wearable tracking stops

Cortisol+ can display available sleep and cardiovascular signals. It cannot determine libido, testosterone, estrogen or cortisol levels, diagnose a sexual health condition or confirm that a supplement worked.

A useful app observation would be “my recorded sleep was shorter this week.” It would not be “my score proves that cortisol lowered my sex hormones.” The [methodology page](/how-it-works/) explains the model's actual inputs and limitations.
