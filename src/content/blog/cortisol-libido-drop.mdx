**A change in sex drive doesn't prove high cortisol or low testosterone.**

It can matter a lot to you, without having a simple hormone answer.

## What could be behind it?

**Look at the whole picture.** The [NHS guide to low libido](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/loss-of-libido/) lists possible causes, including:

- Stress
- Relationships
- Pain or discomfort during sex
- Pregnancy
- Menopause
- Medicines
- Health conditions

Several of these can happen at once.

You may have heard that cortisol "steals" what your body needs to make sex hormones. For one person's symptom, that's an unhelpful shortcut. Neither how you feel nor an app score shows that this is happening in your body.

## How do I talk to a professional about it?

**If the change worries you, talk to a healthcare professional.** You can describe:

- When it started
- Whether it's different from what's usual for you
- Whether there's pain or another sexual concern
- Whether any medicines changed around the same time

**You don't need a watch chart to justify the appointment.**

If the topic feels hard to talk about, a short written note can help you say what you want help with.

Treatment depends on the cause. If you think a medicine is involved, talk to the person who prescribed it before you change anything.

## Where does watch tracking stop?

**Cortisol+ can show your available sleep and heart signals.** It cannot:

- Measure libido
- Measure testosterone, estrogen or cortisol levels
- Diagnose a sexual health condition
- Confirm that a supplement worked

<ZenPicture src="/app-ui/start/zen_explain.webp" caption="Your watch shows signals. It can’t show your hormones." />

A useful app observation would be "my recorded sleep was shorter this week." It would not be "my score proves that cortisol lowered my sex hormones."
