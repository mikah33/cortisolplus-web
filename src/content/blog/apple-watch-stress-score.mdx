## Is there an Apple Watch stress score?

**No. If you're looking for an Apple Watch stress score built into the watch, you won't find one.**

Fitbit and Garmin watches have one. Apple doesn't include a stress measurement or a "body battery" feature.

But your Apple Watch still tracks data linked to stress. Other apps use that data to estimate your stress. Below: what the watch tracks, how apps turn it into a score, and how accurate that is.

## What does the Apple Watch track?

Your Apple Watch records several things that change when you're stressed:

- **Heart rate:** tracked all day and night.
- **Heart rate variability (HRV):** the change in time between heartbeats, measured during sleep and rest.
- **Breathing rate:** breaths per minute while you sleep.
- **Blood oxygen:** SpO2 levels (on Series 6 and newer).
- **Activity and workouts:** how you move and how hard you exercise.
- **Sleep:** time asleep and sleep stages (on watchOS 9 and newer).

None of these is a stress score on its own. But put together with smart formulas, they can give a reasonable estimate of your stress.

## How do other apps make a stress score?

Apps like Cortisol+, Welltory and HRV4Training read your Apple Watch data through HealthKit, Apple's health data system. Then each app uses its own formula.

**Most of them lean on HRV.**

- When you're relaxed, HRV tends to be higher.
- When you're stressed, anxious or overtrained, HRV usually drops.

Apps compare today's HRV with your own usual level (your baseline). Then they flag when you might be under more stress.

Some apps also look at:

- changes in resting heart rate (a higher resting heart rate often signals stress or illness)
- how long and how well you sleep
- how active you are and how you recover
- the time of day and what's going on

Apps differ in how much each thing counts and how they show it. Some give one stress number. Others show trends over time, or labels like "low", "moderate" and "high".

Learn more in our [Apple Watch HRV guide](/hrv/apple-watch/).

## Are these stress scores accurate?

**It depends on the data and the formula.**

Apple Watch heart rate and HRV readings are fairly accurate for a wrist device. Research shows they're close to chest-strap monitors for most people at rest.

They can be less reliable during hard movement. They can also be less reliable for people with darker skin or tattoos on their wrists.

Even with good data, estimating stress is hard:

- Your heart rate might jump because you're stressed, or because you just climbed stairs.
- Your HRV might drop from emotional stress, tiredness, not drinking enough water, or poor sleep.

Good apps try to account for this. They learn your patterns over weeks and months, so they can tell normal ups and downs from real stress signals. But no app is perfect.

**Think of a stress score as one clue, not a diagnosis.** If your score says you're stressed and you feel fine, trust your body. If your score is low but you feel terrible, that matters too.

## Why doesn't Apple build one in?

Apple hasn't made a stress score, probably for a few reasons:

1. **Stress is hard to define and harder to measure.** No single body signal captures every kind of stress: physical, emotional, short-term and long-term.
2. **Mistakes could cause problems.** Telling someone they're stressed when they're not (a false positive), or missing real stress (a false negative), could do harm. Apple tends to be careful with health features.
3. **Stress needs context and personal tuning.** What stresses you might not stress someone else. Apps that focus on stress can tune their formulas faster than Apple can update watchOS.

Apple may add a stress feature one day. It has added sleep tracking, cycle tracking and cardio fitness scores over time. For now, stress measurement is left to other apps.
