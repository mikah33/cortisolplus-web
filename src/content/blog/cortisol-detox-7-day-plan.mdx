## What is a cortisol detox, really?

**You don't need a juice cleanse or detox tea.** But some specific changes over seven days can measurably shift your stress hormone patterns.

A cortisol detox isn't about getting rid of toxins. It's about two things:

- **Removing** the triggers that keep your cortisol high.
- **Adding back** the habits that help it follow its natural rhythm.

Here's what actually works in a week-long plan.

## What does "resetting" cortisol mean?

Cortisol follows a daily pattern. It should rise within 30 to 45 minutes of waking, then slowly fall through the day until it's low at bedtime ([StatPearls, *Physiology, Cortisol*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538239/)).

Long-term stress, poor sleep and some lifestyle habits flatten this curve.

A real cortisol detox aims to bring that natural pattern back. You're not trying to get rid of cortisol. You need it to wake up, handle challenges and control inflammation.

Instead, you're teaching your **HPA axis** (the communication line between your brain and adrenal glands) to stop overreacting.

**Seven days is enough to see early changes** in cortisol patterns. Full recovery from long-term stress can take weeks to months.

## Days 1 and 2: Fix your sleep first

**Not sleeping enough is one of the fastest ways to raise evening cortisol.** Even one poor night raises next-day cortisol.

Your first 48 hours focus on sleep basics:

- Set a fixed wake time, even on weekends, to anchor your body clock
- Get 10+ minutes of bright outdoor light within an hour of waking
- No caffeine after 12 PM
- Make your bedroom completely dark, or wear an eye mask
- Aim for 7 to 9 hours in bed

Morning light matters most. It strengthens the signal that tells cortisol when to rise and when to fall. If you can't get outside, a 10,000-lux light box works.

For more detail, see our guide on [how sleep affects cortisol levels](/cortisol/lower/sleep/).

## Days 3 and 4: Add short calm-down breaks

From day three, add planned breaks that help lower cortisol during the day. Some research suggests even short breaks may reduce cortisol when you do them regularly.

**Breathing exercises are the most reliable tool.** Two to try:

- **Box breathing:** breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4.
- **Long out-breath:** breathe in for 4 seconds, breathe out for 6 to 8.

Both switch on your body's "rest and digest" system (the parasympathetic nervous system). Do 5 minutes mid-morning and 5 minutes mid-afternoon.

Our [breathing exercises for cortisol](/cortisol/lower/breathing-exercises/) guide walks through specific techniques proven to work.

**Other good cortisol breaks:**

- 10 to 20 minute walks at an easy pace (not hard exercise yet)
- Listening to music you find relaxing
- A quick chat with someone you trust

During these breaks, avoid doomscrolling, work emails or anything else that sets off your stress response. The point is to give your HPA axis practice at calming down.

## Days 5 and 6: Adjust food and stimulants

Now sleep and breaks are in place, it's time for the food and drink habits that get in the way of cortisol control.

**Cut back on, or cut out:**

- **Alcohol.** It disrupts the structure of your sleep and raises cortisol overnight.
- **Sugary processed foods.** They spike your blood sugar, and cortisol rises to make up for the crash.
- **Too much caffeine** (more than 200 to 300 mg a day), especially after noon. Caffeine raises cortisol in the short term ([Lovallo et al., 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2257922/)).

**Focus on:**

- **Enough protein at breakfast** (20 to 30 g) to keep blood sugar steady.
- **Regular meals every 3 to 4 hours** to avoid blood sugar crashes.
- **Magnesium-rich foods** like leafy greens, nuts and seeds. Magnesium may slightly reduce feelings of anxiety and stress, though the evidence is limited ([Boyle et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5452159/)).
- **Foods high in vitamin C**, which may support adrenal function. The evidence here is early.

You don't need to eat perfectly. The goal is to remove the biggest cortisol triggers and avoid the blood sugar swings that cause stress hormone spikes.

## Day 7: Check in with yourself

On day seven, take stock. Cortisol changes happen slowly, but you may notice:

- More morning energy, without needing caffeine straight away
- Falling asleep more easily
- Less afternoon anxiety or irritability
- Fewer wake-ups in the middle of the night

Some people feel clearly different after a week. Others see changes in biomarkers (measurable signs in the body) before they feel better.

Use the [free stress and sleep journal](/resources/stress-sleep-journal/) to note what you notice in your own words. A symptom questionnaire **can't estimate your cortisol levels**. It also can't decide whether you need a hormone test.
