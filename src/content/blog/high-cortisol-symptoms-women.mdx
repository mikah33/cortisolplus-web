Weight changes, disrupted sleep or irregular periods should not automatically be labeled high cortisol. A checklist cannot establish the cause, and those symptoms deserve to be discussed on their own terms.

## Cortisol disorders are a specific clinical question

[Cushing's syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cushings-syndrome/) can involve easy bruising, muscle weakness, changes in body-fat distribution, irregular periods and increased hair growth. Symptoms vary, and assessment considers medicines, examination and appropriate testing. This is different from assigning a cortisol diagnosis to common everyday stress symptoms.

## Menopause and other contexts can overlap

The [National Institute on Aging](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/menopause/what-menopause) describes changes in periods, sleep, mood and body composition during the menopause transition. Those overlaps do not establish whether cortisol, menopause or something else explains an individual experience.

The useful next step is to explain what changed and how it affects you, rather than selecting a hormone explanation from a list. A clinician can consider your history and the question a test would answer.

## Before an appointment

Write down the timing and impact of the symptoms, relevant menstrual changes and medicines or supplements. If you already have laboratory results, bring the report and collection time. Do not use the number of checked symptoms as a rule for ordering a cortisol panel.

## What Cortisol+ cannot decide

Cortisol+ does not measure cortisol or reproductive hormones. It cannot distinguish menopause from an endocrine disorder, identify why a period changed or choose hormone therapy. Sleep and HRV records can be personal context, but a score change is not proof of a hormonal cause. Read the [methodology and limits](/how-it-works/).
