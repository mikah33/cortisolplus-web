## Where should a fair comparison start?

**Start with how good the evidence is, not the label.**

Calling an herb an "adaptogen" doesn't show it fixed a cortisol problem. Neither does a low score on your watch. (Cortisol is your body's main stress hormone.)

## What has been studied?

### Ashwagandha

[NCCIH reports](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha) that some forms of ashwagandha may help with stress and trouble sleeping (insomnia).

But the studies are often small, and they use different preparations. So there's no guaranteed percentage drop in cortisol for every product or every person.

### Rhodiola

For [rhodiola, NCCIH concludes](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/rhodiola) there isn't enough reliable evidence to say whether it helps with any health purpose.

It describes most studies in people as low to moderate quality.

An earlier version of this page said rhodiola "wins" for sudden tiredness (acute fatigue). That went further than the evidence does.

## Are they safe? Can I take them together?

**Both need care if you take medicines or have health conditions.**

NCCIH describes side effects in the short term. There's only limited information on long-term safety.

For rhodiola, reported problems include:

- Trouble sleeping.
- A reported interaction with losartan (a blood pressure medicine).
- Safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding is uncertain.

Go over the official summaries with a doctor or pharmacist before you choose a product.

We don't recommend a schedule for taking both, or for "cycling" them. An earlier schedule on this page wasn't backed by any study comparing the two.
