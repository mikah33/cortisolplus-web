Neither an “adaptogen” label nor a low wearable score establishes that an herb treated a cortisol problem. A useful comparison starts with the quality and scope of the evidence.

## What has been studied?

[NCCIH reports](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha) that some ashwagandha preparations may help stress and insomnia, while studies often use small samples and different preparations. This does not support a guaranteed percentage reduction in cortisol for every product or person.

For [rhodiola, NCCIH concludes](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/rhodiola) that reliable evidence is insufficient to determine usefulness for a health-related purpose. It describes most research in people as low to moderate quality. Our earlier blanket claim that rhodiola “wins” for acute fatigue overstated that evidence.

## Compare studies before comparing labels

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Exact extract and preparation | Products sharing an herb name are not necessarily equivalent |
| Who participated | Results in a selected study group may not apply to your situation |
| Outcome measured | Perceived stress, sleep and a laboratory cortisol result are different outcomes |
| Duration and comparator | A short trial does not establish long-term benefit or safety |

## Safety and combinations

Both products require attention to medicines and health conditions. NCCIH describes short-term adverse effects and limited longer-term safety information. For rhodiola, reported issues include sleep disturbance and a reported interaction with losartan; safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding is uncertain. Review the official summaries with a clinician or pharmacist before choosing a product.

We do not recommend a combined dosing schedule or cycling routine. The earlier schedule on this page was not justified by a comparative clinical assessment.

## What tracking cannot prove

Cortisol+ cannot determine which herb is appropriate or whether either changed cortisol. Its wearable score is not a supplement-efficacy test. See the [methodology](/how-it-works/) and our [more detailed ashwagandha review](/cortisol/foods/ashwagandha/) for the distinction between study evidence and product claims.
