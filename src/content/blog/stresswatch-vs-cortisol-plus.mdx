## Should you use StressWatch or Cortisol+?

**Pick StressWatch if you want frequent stress readings on your wrist. Pick Cortisol+ if you want one daily score that combines heart rate variability (HRV), sleep and recovery, with the weights published.**

Both read Apple Health data from an Apple Watch. Neither measures cortisol.

**Disclosure:** Cortisol+ publishes this guide. We compared StressWatch's App Store listing and help documentation, checked October 20, 2026, with our own published methodology. We have not run a hands-on or clinical accuracy comparison of the two apps.

## How does each app make its number?

**StressWatch looks at the last few minutes. Cortisol+ looks at the whole day and night.**

[StressWatch's FAQ](https://docs.ideation.love/stresswatch/en/principle/frequently-asked-questions.html) says its real-time stress level comes from recent heart rate and HRV, and updates about every six minutes.

It shows HRV as a measure called RMSSD. Apple Health's HRV field uses a different one, called SDNN. So its HRV numbers [can differ from Apple Health](https://docs.ideation.love/stresswatch/en/principle/why-are-different-from-apple-health.html). The developer says readings become personal after about 30 days of heart-rate data.

Cortisol+ works out a 0–100 wellness score from up to ten Apple Health inputs. HRV and sleep count 20% each, and resting heart rate 18%. Breathing rate, activity, blood oxygen and other factors make up the rest.

The [methodology page](/how-it-works/) lists every weight, how your baseline builds over your first 28 readings, and what happens when data is missing.

## Where is StressWatch strong?

**StressWatch is popular and puts a stress reading one glance away.**

- It has a 4.7 rating from more than 24,000 US App Store ratings (October 20, 2026).
- Apple has featured it in its editorial collections.
- Its complications (small watch-face widgets) show a stress reading at a glance.

Its documentation is honest about limits. It says the app tracks physical stress, "cannot monitor emotional or cognitive stress", and that HRV is not the same as mental stress. It also lists when readings pause: during workouts, in Low Power Mode, or when the watch is loose.

If you want a frequent, quick signal through the day, StressWatch is designed around that.

<ZenTip image="/app-ui/zen_greet.webp">
  Wear your watch <strong>snug</strong>, not loose. A loose watch can pause readings in apps like StressWatch.
</ZenTip>

## Where is Cortisol+ different?

**Cortisol+ asks a slower question: how do sleep, HRV and recovery fit together from day to day?**

Instead of a stream of minute-by-minute readings, you get one score and the factors that moved it. You can see how each factor is weighted.

That openness lets you check the assumptions. It doesn't prove the score is more accurate. A different formula reading the same Watch data will give a different number, and that alone doesn't show which app is right.

## What do they cost?

**Both apps are free to download and keep their analysis behind a subscription.**

Prices and trial terms vary by account and change over time. Check the offer shown in each app before you subscribe. Cortisol+ analysis requires a subscription or an eligible trial.

## How can I compare them myself?

1. Wear the same Watch with your usual routine for a week, and let both apps read Apple Health.
2. Compare like with like. A StressWatch reading at 3 p.m. isn't the same as a Cortisol+ score for the whole day.
3. When either app shows a surprising value, open Apple Health and check that day's HRV and sleep.
4. Note which explanation actually helps you change something, like bedtime, training load or when you have caffeine. Our free [stress and sleep journal](/resources/stress-sleep-journal/) is a place to write it down.
5. Decide on usefulness and price. A week of comparing screens can't prove anything about hormones.
