**Yes, stress and cortisol can play a part in back pain.**

When you're stressed, your body releases hormones, including cortisol, your main stress hormone ([StatPearls: Physiology, Cortisol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538239/)). These hormones make your muscles tense and your heart beat faster ([MedlinePlus: Stress and your health](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003211.htm)).

That's useful for a short burst. But when stress goes on for weeks, the tension, the poor sleep and the long tense days can all add up in your back.

Here's how it works, what you can do, and when to get help.

## How can stress make my back hurt?

**Stress can tighten your muscles.** Muscle tension is one of the common physical signs of stress ([Cleveland Clinic: Stress](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11874-stress)).

You may not even notice it. People can build up muscle tension without being aware of it ([Cambridge University Hospitals: Stress and anxiety](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/stress-and-anxiety-back-pain-management-programme/)).

Very tense muscles, or muscle spasms, are a common cause of sudden low back pain ([MedlinePlus: Low back pain, acute](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm)).

Stress can also affect your back in other ways:

- **Long, tense days at a desk.** Back pain often starts after sitting in one position for a long time ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm)). Poor sitting posture can make back and neck pain worse ([Cambridge University Hospitals: Seating and ergonomics](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/seating-and-ergonomics/)).
- **Poor sleep.** Stress can cause trouble sleeping ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003211.htm)). And poor sleep and pain feed each other: "pain worsens sleep patterns and sleep disturbances worsen pain" ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/managing-insomnia-for-those-with-chronic-pain/)).
- **Feeling the pain more.** Feelings like stress and frustration can make pain worse ([MedlinePlus: Pain and your emotions](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000417.htm)).

## Can long-term stress keep back pain going?

**It can make pain that's already there harder to shake.**

Stress doesn't cause long-term (chronic) pain by itself. But it can affect it ([Cambridge University Hospitals](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/stress-and-anxiety-back-pain-management-programme/)).

Living with pain is stressful. That stress adds more muscle tension, which makes the pain worse. It can turn into a vicious circle.

Worry plays a part too. If you tend to expect the worst, back pain can feel far worse ([Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/psychology-low-back-pain-201604259537)). Pain, and fear of pain, can also make you avoid activity. Over time that can mean less strength and more pain ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000417.htm)).

The good news: this works in both directions. Calming the stress side can help the pain side.

## What else causes back pain?

**Stress is only one piece.** It's not always obvious what causes back pain, and it often gets better on its own ([NHS: Back pain](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).

Common causes include:

- A pulled or strained muscle
- Lifting something heavy or moving suddenly ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm))
- A slipped disc
- Sciatica (a trapped nerve)
- Medical conditions such as ankylosing spondylitis

Very rarely, back pain can be a sign of something serious, like a broken bone, cancer or an infection ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).

### What about very high cortisol?

**This is rare, and different from everyday stress.**

Cushing's syndrome is a rare condition caused by having too much cortisol in your body for a long time. The most common cause is taking high doses of steroid medicines for a long time ([NHS: Cushing's syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cushings-syndrome/)).

It can weaken bones (osteoporosis). Signs can include backache with routine activities and fractures of the ribs or spine caused by thinning bones ([MedlinePlus: Cushing syndrome](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000410.htm)).

If you take steroid medicine and notice these signs, talk to your doctor. **Don't stop a prescribed steroid on your own.**

## What can I do to ease stress-related back pain?

**Keep moving, and calm the stress side too.** Back pain often improves on its own within a few weeks ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).

### Stay active

- **Keep up your daily activities** as best you can.
- **Don't stay in bed** for long periods. Bed rest is not recommended ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm)).
- **Try gentle activity.** Walking, swimming, yoga and pilates may help ease back pain ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).
- **Try exercises and stretches for back pain.** Stop if the pain gets worse, and see a doctor for advice ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).

<ZenPicture src="/app-ui/zen/zen-walk.webp" caption="A gentle walk is one of the easiest ways to keep your back moving." />

### Ease the pain

- **Heat or ice.** A heat pack can relieve stiffness or muscle spasms. An ice pack can reduce pain and swelling. Wrap either one in a tea towel ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).
- **Painkillers.** Anti-inflammatory medicine like ibuprofen may help, but it isn't suitable for everyone. Check with a pharmacist or doctor if you're unsure ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).

### Set up your desk

These tips come from a back pain leaflet by [Cambridge University Hospitals](https://www.cuh.nhs.uk/patient-information/seating-and-ergonomics/):

- **Sit back in your chair** so it supports your lower back. A small rolled-up towel at the bottom of your back can help.
- **Feet flat** on the floor, or on a footrest.
- **Screen at arm's length**, at eye level, in front of you.
- **Keyboard level with your elbows**, with your arms roughly parallel to the floor.
- **Change position often**, and get up and walk around rather than sitting for long stretches.

### Sleep more comfortably

Try lying curled up on your side with a pillow between your legs. If you sleep on your back, put a pillow or rolled towel under your knees ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm)).

If sleep problems keep going, talk to your doctor. Treating insomnia can help chronic pain ease ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/managing-insomnia-for-those-with-chronic-pain/)).

### Relax your body

**Relaxation can loosen the stress side of back pain.**

- **Breathing exercises, meditation, yoga and muscle relaxation** can help keep stress down ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11874-stress)).
- **Progressive muscle relaxation** means tensing and then relaxing your muscles, one group at a time. It may help some kinds of low back pain ([Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/psychology-low-back-pain-201604259537)).
- **Mindfulness-based stress reduction** and **cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)**, a talking therapy, can help with back pain ([Harvard Health](https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/psychology-low-back-pain-201604259537)). The NHS may offer CBT to help people cope with back pain ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)).

## When should I see a doctor?

### Get help straight away

**Call 999 (UK) or 911 (US), or go to A&E or the emergency room, if you have back pain and:**

- Numbness or loss of feeling around your bottom or genitals
- Loss of bladder or bowel control, or trouble peeing
- Weakness, numbness or tingling in your legs
- Back pain after a fall, accident or injury
- Chest pain

([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/); [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm))

Don't drive yourself. Ask someone to drive you, or call for an ambulance.

### Get an urgent appointment

**Get urgent medical advice the same day (in the UK, an urgent GP appointment or NHS 111) if you have back pain and:**

- A fever, or you feel hot, cold, shivery or generally unwell
- Severe pain that starts suddenly, or pain that is getting worse quickly
- Weight loss you can't explain

([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/); [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007425.htm))

### See a doctor soon

See your doctor (GP) if your back pain ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/back-pain/)):

- Doesn't get better after a few weeks of home care
- Stops you doing your day-to-day activities
- Worries you, or you're struggling to cope
- Doesn't improve with rest, or is worse at night
- Comes with a lump or swelling, or your back has changed shape
- Is between your shoulders, rather than low down

Also talk to a doctor if stress feels overwhelming or is affecting your health ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003211.htm)).
