**Yes, stress and cortisol can play a part in eye twitching.** Stress is one of the most common triggers doctors list for a twitching eyelid.

Cortisol is a hormone made by your adrenal glands. It plays a role in your body's stress response ([MedlinePlus: Cortisol test](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003693.htm)).

Doctors don't point to cortisol itself as the cause. **It's stress as a whole that can trigger or worsen a twitch.** And stress rarely comes alone. It often brings short nights and extra coffee, which are triggers too.

## What is eye twitching?

**An eyelid twitch is a spasm of the eyelid muscle that you can't control** ([MedlinePlus: Eyelid twitch](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000756.htm)).

The most common kind is called **myokymia** (say "my-oh-KIM-ee-uh"). It's a slow, wave-like flutter in the eyelid. It's very common and usually harmless ([Cleveland Clinic: Myokymia](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/myokymia)).

A twitch usually lasts seconds to minutes. For some people it can come and go for hours.

The NHS says twitches like this are common and **rarely a sign of anything serious** ([NHS: Twitching eyes and muscles](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/twitching-eyes-and-muscles/)).

## What sets off an eye twitch?

**Usually it's a mix of everyday things adding up.** Common triggers include ([Cleveland Clinic: Eye twitching](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/17663-eye-twitching); [Cleveland Clinic: Myokymia](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/myokymia)):

- **Stress or anxiety**
- **Tiredness** or not enough sleep
- **Caffeine** (and nicotine)
- **Eye strain**
- **Dry eyes**
- Alcohol
- Bright light
- Some medicines

## How can stress and poor sleep set it off?

**Stress and tiredness are both on the trigger list, and they often feed each other.**

When you're stressed, it can be harder to sleep well. Stress and anxiety are among the most common causes of poor sleep ([NHS: Insomnia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insomnia/)). Then you're tired the next day, so you may reach for more coffee. You might also spend longer staring at a screen to catch up.

That's several triggers stacked together: stress, tiredness, caffeine and eye strain. It's why a twitch often shows up during a hard week and fades once things calm down.

If stress is keeping you up at night, our guide to [cortisol, stress and sleep](/blog/cortisol-sleep-insomnia/) has more on that loop.

## What can I do to stop it?

**Most twitches go away on their own within about a week** ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000756.htm)). These simple steps can help:

1. **Get more sleep.** Rest is the first thing the NHS and MedlinePlus suggest.
2. **Cut back on caffeine.** Don't have large amounts of coffee, tea or energy drinks.
3. **Drink less alcohol.**
4. **Try to relax.** The NHS suggests relaxation techniques, and trying not to worry about the twitch.
5. **Use lubricating eye drops** if your eyes feel dry.
6. **Rest your eyes.** Eye strain is a trigger, so take breaks from long stretches of close-up or screen work.

Don't stop a prescribed medicine on your own. If you think a medicine is causing the twitch, talk to your doctor first ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/twitching-eyes-and-muscles/)).

## When should I see a doctor?

**See a doctor or eye specialist if** ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000756.htm); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/twitching-eyes-and-muscles/); [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/myokymia)):

- The twitch **doesn't go away** after a week, or keeps going for weeks
- Your eyelid **closes fully** when it twitches
- The twitch **spreads to other parts of your face**, or you have twitches in more than one area
- Your **upper eyelid droops**
- Your eye is **red or swollen, or has discharge**
- The area feels **weak or stiff**
- Your **vision changes**, or the twitch gets in the way of seeing
- You think a **medicine** might be causing it
