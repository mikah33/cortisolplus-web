Have you noticed more fullness under your chin after a long, stressful stretch? You may be wondering if cortisol is to blame.

**The short answer is yes, cortisol and stress can play a part.** How big a part depends on what's going on in your body.

Cortisol is your body's main stress hormone. There are two ways it can be linked to a double chin:

- **Very high cortisol over a long time** (a medical condition called Cushing's syndrome) is known to add fat to the face, neck and upper back.
- **Everyday stress** can contribute in a more roundabout way, through weight gain, sleep and eating habits.

Let's look at each one.

## How does very high cortisol change the neck and chin?

**When cortisol stays far too high for a long time, fat moves to the upper body.** This is called Cushing's syndrome.

The NHS lists "more body fat on your neck, upper back, chest and tummy" and "a red, round face" as signs of it ([NHS: Cushing's syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cushings-syndrome/)).

MedlinePlus describes fat collecting "between the shoulders and above collar bones", along with a "round, red, full face (moon face)" ([MedlinePlus: Cushing syndrome](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000410.htm)).

Cleveland Clinic also lists weight gain, "especially in your face and belly", and fatty deposits between the shoulder blades as signs of high cortisol ([Cleveland Clinic: Cortisol](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/22187-cortisol)).

**What causes it?** The most common cause is taking high doses of steroid medicines for a long time. Less often, a growth in the pituitary or adrenal glands makes the body produce too much cortisol ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cushings-syndrome/)).

If your face is getting rounder too, read our guide to [moon face and cortisol](/blog/cortisol-moon-face/).

## How can everyday stress contribute?

**Everyday stress can contribute to a double chin by nudging up your weight.** It works through appetite, sleep and movement, rather than moving fat straight to your chin.

Here's how stress can add up:

- **Bigger appetite.** Harvard Health explains that cortisol "increases appetite, so that people will want to eat more", and "increases storage of unused nutrients as fat" ([Harvard Health: Understanding the stress response](https://www.harvardhealth.org/staying-healthy/understanding-the-stress-response)).
- **Less sleep and less exercise.** The same Harvard article says chronic stress may also add to weight gain indirectly, by cutting into sleep and exercise.
- **Weight shows up everywhere.** Cleveland Clinic notes that as people gain weight, "many gain it evenly all across their body, including in the face and chin area" ([Cleveland Clinic: How to get rid of a double chin](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-get-rid-of-a-double-chin)).

So stress can contribute step by step: more eating, less rest, a bit more weight, and some of that weight settles under the chin.

For more on where stress-related weight tends to go, see [cortisol belly](/blog/cortisol-weight-gain-midsection/).

## What else causes a double chin?

**Many double chins have nothing to do with stress.** These are some of the most common causes:

- **Genetics.** "Some people inherit a stronger jawline, while others have a softer one, even if they don't have obesity or overweight" ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-get-rid-of-a-double-chin)). Family history can matter too ([Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/318131)).
- **Weight.** Gaining weight in general can add fat under the chin.
- **Ageing.** Skin loses some of its stretch (elasticity) as we age. This can look like extra or saggy skin under the chin ([Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/318131)).
- **Loose skin.** Loose skin under the chin can create the look of a double chin on its own ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/how-to-get-rid-of-a-double-chin)).
- **Posture.** Poor posture may weaken the neck and chin muscles over time, which may add to a double chin ([Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/318131)).

Many people with fullness under the chin aren't overweight at all. Cleveland Clinic notes it can be stubborn fat that diet and exercise alone don't remove ([Cleveland Clinic: Kybella](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/cosmetic-plastic-surgery/procedures/kybella)).

## How can I tell which one it is?

**Look at the bigger picture, not just your chin.**

A double chin on its own, that's been there a long time or runs in your family, is most likely down to genes, weight or age.

Changes linked to Cushing's syndrome rarely come alone. The NHS also lists signs like:

- thin arms and legs, with more fat on the tummy
- bruising easily
- weak muscles
- mood changes
- irregular periods

([NHS: Cushing's syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cushings-syndrome/))

## When should I see a doctor?

**See a doctor if your face, neck or upper back change along with other signs** like the ones above. This matters most if you take steroid medicine.

The NHS says to "see a GP if you have symptoms of Cushing's syndrome, particularly if you're taking steroid medicine" ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cushings-syndrome/)).

A doctor can decide whether [cortisol testing](/cortisol/test/) makes sense, and which test to use.

**Never stop a prescribed steroid on your own.** The NHS says, "Do not stop taking your steroid medicine without talking to your doctor first" ([NHS: Steroids](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/steroids/)).
