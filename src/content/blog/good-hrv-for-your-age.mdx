## What's a good HRV for my age?

**There's no single "good" number.** Age averages exist, but your own normal and your own trends tell you much more.

Heart rate variability (HRV) is the small change in time between your heartbeats. You might track it on an Apple Watch or another device.

Comparing yourself with strangers tells you less about your stress and recovery than comparing yourself with you.

Here's what research shows about HRV at different ages, and why your own pattern matters more.

## Average HRV by age group

**HRV naturally goes down as you get older.** That's because your autonomic nervous system (the part that runs things like your heartbeat on its own) becomes less flexible over time.

Here are average RMSSD values (a common way to measure HRV) from one large study of 1,906 healthy adults. Each is the average ± standard deviation, in milliseconds (ms), for women and men:

- **Ages 25-34:** women 42.9 ± 22.8 ms, men 39.7 ± 19.9 ms
- **Ages 35-44:** women 35.4 ± 18.5 ms, men 32.0 ± 16.5 ms
- **Ages 45-54:** women 26.3 ± 13.6 ms, men 23.0 ± 10.9 ms
- **Ages 55-64:** women 21.4 ± 11.9 ms, men 19.9 ± 11.1 ms
- **Ages 65-74:** women 19.1 ± 11.8 ms, men 19.1 ± 10.7 ms

Source: [Voss et al., "Short-Term Heart Rate Variability — Influence of Gender and Age in Healthy Subjects," PLOS ONE, 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118308), Tables 5 and 7. These came from 5-minute ECG recordings taken lying down after a rest. The study didn't include people under 25 or over 74.

The big standard deviations show how much healthy people of the same age differ. Our [HRV by age chart](/hrv/normal-range/) has the full table and its limits.

### People in the same age group vary a lot

A 30-year-old endurance athlete might have a much higher HRV than a 30-year-old who doesn't exercise. Both could be "normal" for them.

In the study above, women and men had similar averages in every age group.

Your fitness, sleep, stress and genes all affect where you fall in the [normal range](/hrv/normal-range/).

## Why does my own normal matter more?

**What really matters is your HRV compared with your own baseline (your usual level).**

Comparing yourself with averages for your age misses the point. Here's why:

- If your normal HRV is 40 ms and it drops to 25 ms, that's worth noticing. It can be a sign of stress, poor recovery or overtraining.
- If someone else's HRV is always 25 ms and stays there, they're fine.

### Think of it like blood pressure

[HRV](/hrv/) is a bit like blood pressure. One person might normally run at 110/70, another at 125/80. Both are healthy.

But if the first person suddenly jumps to 140/90, something changed. The trend matters more than the number itself.

### How to find your baseline

Track your HRV for at least two weeks. Then look for patterns:

- Does your HRV drop after alcohol or poor sleep?
- Does it rise on rest days?
- Do you feel worse when your HRV is low?

These links in your own data are far more useful than worrying about whether you match a chart.

## What else affects HRV besides age?

Age isn't the only thing. These help explain why your number might not match the averages above:

- **Fitness:** regular aerobic exercise (like brisk walking, running or cycling) raises HRV over time. Endurance athletes often have much higher HRV than people their age who don't exercise.
- **Sleep:** poor sleep can lower HRV, sometimes as soon as the next morning.
- **Stress:** long-term stress can keep your "fight or flight" system (the sympathetic nervous system) switched on. That can lower HRV. This is why HRV is often used as a sign of stress. It can't tell you your cortisol level.
- **Training load:** hard workouts lower HRV for a while. That's normal. But if it stays low for days, you might be overtraining.
- **Illness:** being sick can lower your HRV.
- **Time of day:** HRV changes through the day. That's why many people measure it at the same time each day, often first thing in the morning.
- **How it's measured:** wrist sensors are handy but less accurate than chest straps. Your device matters.

## When should I worry about low HRV?

**One low reading isn't a crisis.** HRV goes up and down every day. Patterns that keep showing up deserve attention.

You might want to look into it if:

- Your HRV stays well below your baseline for several days
- You feel exhausted or irritable, or can't recover from workouts
- Your HRV keeps going down week after week
- Low HRV comes with other symptoms, like chest pain or dizziness

Low HRV often means your body needs rest. For athletes, that might mean a recovery day. For everyone else, it might mean working on sleep, stress or daily habits.

### Is high HRV always better?

Not always. A higher number isn't automatically a sign of better health. If a reading looks unusual for you, look at it alongside how you feel and your recent days.
