## Oura Daytime Stress

**Oura says Daytime Stress works on Gen3 rings and later, with an active membership.**

Its graph updates while you're awake, wearing the ring and fairly still. Activity, sleep and recording problems can leave gaps.

These are limits Oura documents, not results from our own testing. See [Oura's Daytime Stress documentation](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/21205822135315-Daytime-Stress).

<ZenTip image="/app-ui/zen_new_low.webp">
  Gaps in a daytime stress graph are normal. Most trackers only read stress <strong>while you're fairly still</strong>.
</ZenTip>

## WHOOP Stress Monitor

**WHOOP has its own Stress Monitor.**

WHOOP describes a 0–3 scale based on heart rate and HRV compared with your personal baseline. It also accounts for movement. WHOOP also describes stress history and breathing features.

We said earlier that WHOOP didn't have a stress-specific measure. **That was wrong.** See [WHOOP's feature explanation](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/introducing-stress-monitor-a-new-way-to-monitor-manage-stress/).

## Apple Watch with Cortisol+

Apple Watch saves HRV as a measure called **SDNN**, and it only records in the background now and then. Opening an app doesn't guarantee a new HRV reading.

**Cortisol+ uses the records that are available, plus model assumptions. It has no cortisol sensor.** See our [Apple Watch guide](/stress/apple-watch/) and [full method](/how-it-works/).

The app is free to download. The analysis needs a subscription or an eligible trial. Optional connected services send data in more ways than the on-device score does. Check the [privacy policy](/privacy/) and the offer shown in the app.

## How do I compare them fairly?

Compare:

- the device you'll actually wear (ring, strap or watch)
- the records you can look at yourself
- how each one handles missing data
- the full current hardware-plus-membership offer where you live

We've removed old prices, battery figures and unsupported "best accuracy" rankings from this page.

**A company's stress score is not a standard hormone result.**

- Don't compare SDNN and RMSSD numbers as if they were the same measure.
- A product that updates more often hasn't shown better cortisol accuracy just by doing so.
