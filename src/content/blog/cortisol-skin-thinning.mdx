**Thin skin, or skin that bruises easily, doesn't prove that everyday stress damaged your collagen.**

How your skin looks can't show your cortisol level. It also can't tell you which treatment you need.

## What else could explain it?

**There's more than one possible reason.**

Skin changes with age. Sun exposure also changes how skin looks and how it's built. [MedlinePlus explains aging skin](https://medlineplus.gov/skinaging.html). These shouldn't be brushed aside for a single stress-hormone story.

**Steroid creams can thin the skin.** This is more likely with stronger creams or long-term use. It depends on how and where the medicine is used.

The [joint dermatology and eczema-society statement](https://eczema.org/wp-content/uploads/Topical_Steroid_Topical_Withdrawal_Joint_Statement_Feb_2024.pdf) discusses these effects. It also stresses that steroid treatment, used the right way, can be useful and work well.

## What should I tell my clinician?

**Bring your real medicine history.** Tell your clinician about any:

- Creams
- Tablets
- Inhalers
- Injections

Include products you bought without a prescription.

For creams, include the name, where on your body you use it, and roughly how long you've used it.

Ask whether the change needs to be examined, and whether the way you use your medicine is still right.

**Talk to your clinician before changing prescribed steroid treatment.** A wellness score can't tell you whether a medicine caused a skin problem, or whether it's safe to stop.

## Can it be reversed?

**Be wary of recovery promises.** Whether skin changes get better, and how long that takes, depends on the cause.

A general collagen supplement, a cortisol routine or an app trend can't give you a recovery timeline.

**New unexplained bruising, skin injury or other worrying changes need to be checked on their own.**
