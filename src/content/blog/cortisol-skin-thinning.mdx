Thin or easily bruised skin does not prove that everyday stress damaged your collagen. A visual change cannot establish your cortisol level or identify the treatment you need.

## More than one explanation

Skin changes with age, and sun exposure contributes to changes in its appearance and structure. [MedlinePlus explains aging skin](https://medlineplus.gov/skinaging.html). These factors should not be dismissed in favor of a single stress-hormone story.

Topical corticosteroids can cause skin thinning, particularly with stronger preparations or prolonged use. That possibility depends on how and where a medicine is used. The [joint dermatology and eczema-society statement](https://eczema.org/wp-content/uploads/Topical_Steroid_Topical_Withdrawal_Joint_Statement_Feb_2024.pdf) discusses these effects while emphasizing that appropriate steroid treatment can be useful and effective.

## Bring the actual medication history

Tell your clinician which creams, tablets, inhalers or injections you use, including nonprescription products. For creams, include the name, body area and approximate duration. Ask whether the change needs examination and whether your current use remains appropriate.

Discuss concerns before changing prescribed steroid treatment. A wellness score cannot tell you whether a medicine caused a skin problem or whether it is safe to stop.

## Avoid recovery promises

Whether skin changes improve, and how long that takes, depends on their cause. A generic collagen supplement, cortisol routine or app trend cannot establish a reversal timeline. New unexplained bruising, skin injury or other concerning changes deserve an assessment of their own.

Cortisol+ does not measure collagen, skin thickness or cortisol. Its [wellness model](/how-it-works/) should not be used to select a skin treatment or judge whether one worked.
