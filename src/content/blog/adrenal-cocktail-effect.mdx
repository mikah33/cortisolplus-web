## What is an adrenal cocktail?

You've probably seen the videos. Someone mixes orange juice, coconut water and sea salt, says it "supports your adrenals", and promises you'll feel calmer and more energized.

The **adrenal cocktail** has millions of views. But does it do anything for your cortisol?

It usually has three ingredients:

- Orange juice (vitamin C)
- Coconut water or cream of tartar (potassium)
- Sea salt (sodium)

The theory is that these nutrients "support adrenal function" and help your body handle stress. Some versions add magnesium or other supplements.

The drink spread through wellness influencers who link it to "adrenal fatigue". They say modern stress wears out your adrenal glands, and this cocktail gives them what they need to recover.

**There's just one problem: adrenal fatigue isn't a recognized medical condition.**

## Is adrenal fatigue real?

**No.** The Endocrine Society, which represents thousands of hormone specialists, has said clearly that adrenal fatigue is not a real diagnosis.

A systematic review of the evidence found no support for "adrenal fatigue" as a genuine medical condition ([Cadegiani & Kater, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4997656/)). Your adrenal glands don't get "tired" from normal stress.

Real adrenal insufficiency does exist. It's called Addison's disease. It's serious, rare and diagnosed with blood tests ([StatPearls, *Physiology, Cortisol*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538239/)). It isn't caused by being busy or stressed, and a drink won't fix it.

What people call adrenal fatigue is usually long-term stress, poor sleep or other health problems that deserve a proper check-up. **The symptoms are real. The explanation is wrong.**

## When might it actually help?

**It might make you feel better in some situations, but not because it "supports your adrenals".**

You might benefit if:

- You're dehydrated and low on electrolytes from exercise, heat or illness
- You have very low blood pressure and need more sodium
- You're truly short of these nutrients (rare on a balanced diet)
- Making it helps you pause and relax

The placebo effect is real too. If you believe something will help and it's harmless, you might feel better. That's not nothing. But it's not the same as the drink changing your cortisol.

## What actually helps manage cortisol?

Instead of following trends, focus on what research supports:

- **Sleep.** Poor sleep directly raises cortisol. Getting 7 to 9 hours regularly is more powerful than any supplement.
- **Regular meals.** Skipping meals or cutting calories hard raises cortisol. Balanced meals help keep it steady. See our guide to [foods that support healthy cortisol levels](/cortisol/lower/foods/).
- **Movement.** Both too much and too little exercise affect cortisol. Moderate activity helps. Overtraining raises it.
- **Stress management.** Deep breathing, meditation and time in nature have real evidence behind them.
- **Social connection.** Loneliness raises cortisol. Real relationships help.

These aren't trendy or Instagram-worthy. **But they work.**

## Should I try it?

**It probably won't hurt you, unless you have kidney problems or need to watch your salt.** It's basically juice with salt.

But don't expect it to fix burnout, anxiety or long-term stress. Those need real solutions, not a drink recipe.

If you enjoy it and it suits your health, go ahead. Just don't think you're treating a medical condition.

**Don't use it instead of seeing a doctor** if you have worrying symptoms like extreme tiredness, weight changes or dizziness.

The appeal makes sense. When you're exhausted and overwhelmed, you want a simple fix. A quick drink sounds easier than changing your sleep or tackling the real causes of stress. But our bodies don't work that way.
