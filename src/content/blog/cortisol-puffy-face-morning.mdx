A puffy face in a morning photo cannot tell you your cortisol level. “Cortisol face” is not a diagnosis you can confirm from an appearance comparison or a wellness score.

## Swelling deserves its own description

[MedlinePlus describes facial swelling](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003105.htm) as fluid building up in facial tissues. Causes can include allergic reactions, medicine reactions, infection and injury. A clinician needs the history and examination to determine what is relevant.

The more useful questions are whether swelling began suddenly, whether it is painful or worsening, and whether other symptoms are present. There is no universal number of minutes after waking that separates normal from abnormal swelling.

## When to get help

Facial swelling with breathing difficulty needs emergency care. Sudden, severe or painful swelling, or swelling with fever, redness or tenderness, also warrants prompt medical attention. Persistent or worsening swelling should be assessed. These distinctions come from the [MedlinePlus care guidance](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003105.htm).

## What to record

For an appointment, note when the change began, where it occurs and any recent medicines, exposures or injuries. Describe the change in your own words. Do not label it a cortisol disorder because it resembles a social-media image.

## Can Cortisol+ clarify the cause?

No. Cortisol+ does not analyze facial swelling or measure hormone concentrations. Sleep and HRV records may be part of your personal wellness notes, but they cannot explain a facial change.
