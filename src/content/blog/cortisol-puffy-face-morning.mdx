**A puffy face in a morning photo can't tell you your cortisol level.**

"Cortisol face" is not a diagnosis you can confirm by comparing photos or checking a wellness score.

## What causes facial swelling?

**Facial swelling is fluid building up in the tissues of your face.** That's how [MedlinePlus describes facial swelling](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003105.htm).

Causes can include:

- Allergic reactions
- Reactions to medicines
- Infection
- Injury

A clinician needs your history and an exam to work out what matters.

More useful questions are:

- Did the swelling start suddenly?
- Is it painful, or getting worse?
- Do you have other symptoms?

There's no set number of minutes after waking that separates normal swelling from abnormal swelling.

## What should I write down?

**For an appointment, note:**

- When the change started
- Where on your face it happens
- Any recent medicines, things you were exposed to, or injuries

Describe the change in your own words. Don't call it a cortisol disorder because it looks like a picture on social media.

## Can Cortisol+ tell me the cause?

**No.** Cortisol+ doesn't look at facial swelling or measure hormone levels.

Sleep and HRV (heart rate variability) records can be part of your personal wellness notes. But they can't explain a change in your face.
