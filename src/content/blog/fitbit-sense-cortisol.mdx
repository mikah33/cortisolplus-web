## Does the Fitbit Sense measure cortisol?

**No.** The Fitbit Sense offers advanced stress tracking, but it doesn't measure cortisol.

"Fitbit Sense cortisol tracking" is a common mix-up. Fitbit sells stress management features, but the watch doesn't read your cortisol levels. Instead, it uses an **electrodermal activity (EDA) sensor**, which tracks something completely different.

Knowing what your watch really measures matters if you're trying to manage stress or follow your stress hormone patterns. Here's what the Sense does, what it doesn't, and what that means for you.

## What does the EDA sensor measure?

The Fitbit Sense has an EDA sensor on its face. During a guided session, you put your palm over it. It then measures **tiny electrical changes in your skin**.

These changes come from your sweat glands. Sweat glands get more active when your sympathetic nervous system (your "fight or flight" side) switches on.

EDA has a few names:

- electrodermal activity (EDA)
- galvanic skin response (GSR)
- skin conductance

The sensor picks up tiny amounts of sweat, before you'd ever feel sweaty. Your sweat glands are run by your autonomic nervous system, the same system behind fight or flight. When you feel stressed, anxious or emotionally stirred up, your sweat glands get busier. The sensor sees this as higher skin conductance.

**The key point: EDA shows your fight-or-flight activity in real time.** It's a snapshot of how stirred up you are at one moment, while you sit still with your hand on the sensor.

## How is EDA different from cortisol?

Cortisol is a hormone made by your adrenal glands. It follows a daily rhythm. It usually peaks 30 to 45 minutes after you wake up, then falls through the day.

Cortisol does respond to stress, but more slowly than your nervous system.

- **EDA reacts in seconds.** When something startles you or you feel anxious, your skin conductance changes within moments.
- **Cortisol reacts over minutes to hours.** It rises 15 to 30 minutes after a stressor starts and can stay high for hours.

Think of EDA as your body's alarm going off. Cortisol is more like the hormones that help you deal with the emergency over the next few hours. Both are linked to stress, but they're completely different body processes on different timescales.

The [Fitbit Stress Management Score](/fitbit/stress-management-score/) mixes EDA readings with other data, like heart rate variability (HRV), sleep and activity. That gives a broader picture of your stress load. But it still doesn't measure cortisol.

## Why can't a watch measure cortisol?

**No consumer wearable can measure cortisol from your wrist right now.**

Cortisol is in your blood, saliva and urine. It isn't on your skin in amounts a watch can measure. The only proven ways to test cortisol use these body fluids:

- **Blood tests**, usually done in the morning
- **Saliva samples**, often taken at several times through the day
- **Urine collection**, usually over 24 hours

Some research companies are working on sweat-based cortisol sensors. But they aren't for sale, and they face big technical problems. Sweat has much less cortisol than saliva or blood, so it's harder to measure accurately.

Fitbit has never claimed to measure cortisol. The confusion comes from its stress-tracking marketing, which some people read as cortisol measurement.

## What does Fitbit Sense stress data tell you?

Even without cortisol, the EDA sensor and Stress Management Score can still be useful.

**The EDA scan shows fight-or-flight activity.** High readings during a scan suggest your body is more stirred up. Lower readings suggest you're more relaxed. This can help you learn which activities or breathing exercises really calm you down.

**The Stress Management Score mixes several signals.** Fitbit looks at your EDA sessions, HRV, sleep quality and activity, and gives you a daily score. Lower scores suggest a higher overall stress load. Over time, this score might line up with your cortisol patterns, but it's not measuring the hormone itself.

**Patterns matter more than single readings.** One high EDA reading doesn't mean much. But steadily high readings, or Stress Management Scores falling over weeks, might mean your stress load is growing. That could line up with changes in your cortisol patterns, even though you're not measuring cortisol.
