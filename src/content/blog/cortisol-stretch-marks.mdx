**Purple stretch marks don't automatically mean high cortisol.**

Their color, where they are, or how they look in a photo can't give you a hormone diagnosis.

## Why do stretch marks appear?

**Stretch marks are scars from skin stretching or shrinking quickly.** That's how the [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/cosmetic/scars-stretch-marks/stretch-marks-why-appear) (AAD) describes them.

They can happen with:

- Puberty
- Pregnancy
- Weight changes
- Fast muscle growth

Using steroid creams for a long time, and some medical conditions, can also play a part.

**Color alone can't tell you the cause.** New marks may be pink, red, purple or brown, depending on your skin color. They often change over time. So color can't separate ordinary marks from a medical cause.

## When does the bigger picture matter?

**If marks are new and unexplained, or come with other changes that worry you, talk to a clinician about the whole picture.**

Note:

- When they appeared
- Recent changes to your body
- Medicines, including steroid creams

A cortisol quiz can't decide whether you need testing.

**Avoid two opposite shortcuts:**

- Thinking every purple mark is a hormone disorder.
- Thinking someone is healthy because their marks don't match a checklist.

## What can treatment do?

**Treatments may make stretch marks less noticeable. They don't reliably erase them,** AAD notes. No single treatment works for everyone.

Ask about options that suit your skin and situation. Don't use a cortisol-lowering routine as a scar treatment.
