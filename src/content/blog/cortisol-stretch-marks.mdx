Most people get stretch marks from growth spurts, pregnancy, or weight changes. But in rare cases, **cortisol stretch marks** signal something more serious happening in your body. The difference comes down to what your cortisol is actually doing and for how long.

Cortisol is your main stress hormone. Your adrenal glands release it when you're stressed, when you wake up, and throughout the day in a natural rhythm. Normal cortisol helps you handle challenges. But when cortisol stays extremely high for months or years, it starts breaking down the proteins that keep your skin strong. That's when cortisol stretch marks can appear.

This post explains when stretch marks actually connect to cortisol problems, what makes them different from regular stretch marks, and when you should talk to a doctor.

## What makes cortisol stretch marks different

Regular stretch marks happen when skin stretches faster than the collagen and elastin fibers can handle. They start out pink or purple, then fade to white or silver over time. You'll find them on hips, thighs, breasts, and bellies during puberty, pregnancy, or rapid muscle gain.

Cortisol stretch marks look and behave differently:

- **Width**: They're often wider than a pencil, sometimes several centimeters across
- **Color**: Deep purple or dark red, and they stay that color instead of fading
- **Location**: They show up in unusual spots like the face, armpits, upper arms, and shoulders
- **Skin changes**: The surrounding skin often looks thin, bruises easily, or heals slowly
- **Pattern**: They appear even without weight gain or growth spurts

The key difference is that high cortisol breaks down collagen throughout your body, not just where skin is stretching. This creates wider, darker marks in places that wouldn't normally develop stretch marks.

## When cortisol causes stretch marks

You need extremely elevated cortisol for a long time before it affects your skin this way. We're talking about levels 3-5 times higher than normal, sustained for months.

The most common medical cause is **Cushing's syndrome**, where your body produces too much cortisol or you're exposed to high doses of corticosteroid medications. About 70-80% of people with Cushing's develop the characteristic wide, purple stretch marks. This condition is rare, affecting roughly 10-15 people per million.

Long-term use of corticosteroid medications like prednisone can also cause these changes. People taking high doses for asthma, autoimmune conditions, or organ transplants sometimes develop cortisol-related skin thinning and stretch marks.

Everyday stress does not cause cortisol stretch marks. Even chronic stress that raises your cortisol somewhat won't break down collagen this way. The hormone elevation needs to be severe and constant.

## Other signs to watch for

Cortisol stretch marks rarely appear alone. If your cortisol is high enough to damage your skin, you'll likely notice other [symptoms](/cortisol/symptoms) too:

- Round, full face (often called "moon face")
- Fat deposits on the upper back and neck
- Thin arms and legs with muscle weakness
- Easy bruising from minor bumps
- Wounds that heal slowly
- Purple or red facial flushing
- High blood pressure
- New or worsening diabetes
- Irregular periods or loss of periods
- Mood changes including depression and anxiety

In children, high cortisol typically slows growth while causing weight gain, creating an unusual pattern that pediatricians recognize.

The combination of wide purple stretch marks with several of these other signs is what signals a potential cortisol problem worth investigating.

## When to see a doctor

See a healthcare provider if you develop:

- Wide, dark purple stretch marks in unusual locations
- Stretch marks plus several other symptoms from the list above
- Stretch marks while taking corticosteroid medications
- Stretch marks in a child along with slowed growth

Your doctor will likely start with blood and urine tests to measure your cortisol levels at different times of day. Cortisol normally peaks in the morning and drops at night. In Cushing's syndrome, this rhythm disappears and levels stay high around the clock.

Additional testing might include saliva tests, a dexamethasone suppression test, or imaging to check your pituitary and adrenal glands.

Don't panic if you have regular stretch marks plus stress. Garden-variety stress, even if it feels overwhelming, doesn't cause the kind of cortisol elevation that damages skin. If you're not developing the other physical signs listed above, your stretch marks almost certainly have a normal cause.
