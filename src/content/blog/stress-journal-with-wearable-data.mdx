## What is a stress journal with wearable data?

**It's a short daily note about your day, kept next to the numbers your watch already records.**

Your watch logs heart rate variability (HRV), resting heart rate and sleep on its own. The journal adds what the numbers can't see: a late coffee, a hard meeting, a bad night, a long walk.

A week or two later, you can line the two up and spot patterns.

It's a way to notice things, not a test. Neither a journal nor a wellness score measures cortisol or proves why a number changed.

## When should you write?

**Twice a day works for most people.**

- **Morning:** one line about your sleep and how you feel.
- **Evening:** one line about the day.

If something stands out, like a stressful call or a calm walk, add the time. Then you can find the same moment in Apple Health or the Google Health app later.

<ZenTip image="/app-ui/zen_new_low.webp">
  Don't copy your watch numbers down as you go. They're <strong>already saved</strong>, and checking all day can make you more anxious, not less.
</ZenTip>

## How do you review a week?

**Once a week, put your notes next to your watch data. Look for repeats, not one-off days.**

1. Open Apple Health (or the Google Health app) and look at the week's HRV, resting heart rate and sleep.
2. Mark the days that stand out, good or bad.
3. Read your notes for those days and the evening before. Late caffeine, alcohol, a late meal or a short night often show up the next morning. Our [alcohol, HRV and sleep guide](/blog/alcohol-hrv-sleep/) explains why.
4. Note anything that repeats on two or three separate occasions. A single day rarely means much.
5. Pick one small change to try next week, like an earlier last coffee, and keep logging.

## What can a journal tell you, and what can't it?

**It can show you links. It can't prove causes.**

If your HRV is lower after nights you marked as stressful, stress may have played a part. So could illness, not drinking enough water, a hard workout, alcohol or travel. The journal can't separate those for you.

Watch readings also have gaps. A loose strap, a missed night, or a sensor that only records at certain times all change what you see. That's why the journal has a column for missing data.

A journal and a wellness score can't measure hormones or diagnose anything. Measuring cortisol needs a lab test; see [cortisol testing options](/cortisol/test/). If symptoms last or worry you, talk to a clinician, whatever your numbers say.

## Make it stick

- **Start with three things:** sleep, energy and one event that stood out. Add more only if it stays easy.
- **Keep it in one place:** the printable journal, a notes app or the spreadsheet, whichever you'll open every day.
- **Review weekly, not daily.** Patterns need a week or two to show.
- **Aim to notice, not to hit targets.** The point is to understand your routine, not to chase a number.

<ZenTip image="/app-ui/zen_new_normal.webp">
  Keep the journal <strong>next to your bed</strong> or on your phone's home screen, so the morning line is quick to write.
</ZenTip>
