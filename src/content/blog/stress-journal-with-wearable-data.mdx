## What is a stress journal with wearable data?

**It's a short daily note about your day, kept alongside the numbers your watch already records.** Your watch logs heart rate variability (HRV), resting heart rate and sleep on its own. The journal adds what the numbers can't see: a late coffee, a hard meeting, a bad night, a long walk. A week or two later you can line the two up and spot patterns.

It's a way to notice things, not a test. Neither a journal nor a wellness score measures cortisol or proves why a number changed.

An example entry: *"Tuesday: bed 11:30 p.m., up 7:00 a.m., felt tired on waking. Busy afternoon, coffee at 3 p.m., short evening walk. Overnight HRV recorded; resting heart rate a little higher than usual."*

## When should you write?

**Twice a day works for most people: a line in the morning about sleep and how you feel, and a line in the evening about the day.** If something stands out, such as a stressful call or a calm walk, add the time. Then you can find the same window in Apple Health or the Google Health app later.

Don't write down your watch numbers live as you go. They're already stored, often with a delay, and checking them all day can make you more anxious, not less.

## How do you review a week?

**Once a week, put your notes next to your watch data and look for repeats, not one-off days.**

1. Open Apple Health (or the Google Health app) and look at the week's HRV, resting heart rate and sleep.
2. Mark the days that stand out in either direction.
3. Read your notes for those days and the evening before. Late caffeine, alcohol, a late meal or a short night often show up the next morning; our [alcohol, HRV and sleep guide](/blog/alcohol-hrv-sleep/) explains why.
4. Note anything that repeats on two or three separate occasions. A single day is rarely meaningful.
5. Pick one small change to try next week, such as an earlier last coffee, and keep logging.

## What can a journal tell you, and what can't it?

**It can show you associations; it can't prove causes.** If your HRV is lower after nights you noted as stressful, stress may have played a part. So could illness, dehydration, a hard workout, alcohol or travel, and the journal can't separate those for you.

Wearable readings also have gaps. A loose strap, a missed night or a sensor that only records at certain times all affect what you see. That's why the journal has a column for missing data.

A journal and a wellness score can't measure hormones or diagnose anything. Measuring cortisol needs a lab test; see [cortisol testing options](/cortisol/test/). If symptoms persist or worry you, talk to a clinician whatever your numbers say.

## Make it stick

- **Start with three things:** sleep, energy and one notable event. Add more only if it stays easy.
- **Keep it in one place:** the printable journal, a notes app or the spreadsheet, whichever you'll open daily.
- **Review weekly, not daily.** Patterns need a week or two to show.
- **Aim for awareness, not targets.** The point is to understand your routine, not to chase a number.
