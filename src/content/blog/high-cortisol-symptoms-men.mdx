**Feeling tired, having a lower sex drive or seeing your body shape change does not prove a man has high cortisol.**

These changes can have several explanations. A wearable stress score can't tell them apart.

## Symptoms are a starting point, not an answer

**Sexual, mood and energy changes have many possible causes.**

The [NHS page on male midlife symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/male-menopause/) explains that these changes may relate to:

- Psychological factors
- Lifestyle
- Medical conditions

Low testosterone (testosterone deficiency) is one possible question for a clinician to look at. It's not a conclusion you can reach just because you feel tired or stressed.

You may hear that cortisol and testosterone work like a seesaw: one goes up, the other goes down. **That simple idea can't diagnose what's happening in one person.** Don't choose supplements or hormone treatment based on it.

  Before your appointment, <strong>jot down when things changed</strong> and how they affect your day. It makes the visit much more useful.

## When is cortisol part of a medical check?

**When a clinician is looking at a specific condition, such as Cushing's syndrome.**

Cushing's syndrome can include muscle weakness, bruising easily and changes in where body fat is stored. Men may also have sexual or fertility changes. [NIDDK describes the condition](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome).

**A list of symptoms is not enough to diagnose it.**

## How do I prepare useful information?

**Bring the facts that help a clinician ask the right question:**

- When the changes began
- Whether they affect your daily life or training
- Any medicines or supplements you take
- Actual test results, if you already have them

Ask what the clinician is looking for before a hormone test is ordered or read.

## What can wearable trends add?

**Context, not a diagnosis.**

You can look at sleep or HRV (heart rate variability) records as background. But an app cannot show that cortisol lowered your testosterone. It can't confirm you've recovered from a hormone problem either.

Cortisol+ has **no validated threshold** for diagnosing overtraining or a testosterone disorder. See [how the wellness score works](/how-it-works/) before using it in a training decision.
