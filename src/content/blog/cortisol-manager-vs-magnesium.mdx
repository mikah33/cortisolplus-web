## How are they different?

Cortisol Manager is a branded supplement with several ingredients in it.

Magnesium is a mineral. It's sold in different forms, and mixed with other things.

Comparing the two doesn't show that either one treats a cortisol disorder. (Cortisol is your body's main stress hormone.)

## Why should I start with the label?

**Because the exact bottle matters.**

The [manufacturer's Cortisol Manager page](https://integrativepro.com/products/cortisol-manager) shows its current label. It also explains the difference between the standard formula and an allergen-free version.

The standard formula contains phosphatidylserine made from soy.

Check the bottle you'd actually use. Look at:

- the serving size
- the ingredients
- the warnings

### What we changed on this page

An earlier version of this article gave doses for each ingredient. It also suggested a stronger do-it-yourself mix. We removed that advice.

A study of one extract doesn't show what a different blend will do. And a higher dose isn't proof of a better result.

## How is magnesium different?

[NIH's magnesium fact sheet](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Magnesium-Consumer/) explains what magnesium does in your body and what to think about with supplements.

You may see "magnesium stearate" listed as an inactive ingredient in other products. That's **not** the same as a supplement that's meant to give you magnesium.

Your body needs magnesium to work normally. That doesn't make it the "strongest cortisol intervention."

Choose a product based on the real food or health question you have.

## What should I ask a pharmacist or doctor?

- Is there a reason for me to take this product?
- Does this exact formula interact with my medicines or other supplements?
- What result would we look at, and how would we check it?
- What side effects or changes should I tell you about?

Bring the current label with you.

Marketing, research on single ingredients, and medical evidence for the finished product are three different things.

This page compares labels and evidence. It's not a recommendation to start either one.
