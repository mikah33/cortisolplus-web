## How are Garmin and Fitbit different?

**Comparing Garmin vs Fitbit stress tracking means comparing two very different ideas.**

- **Garmin's Body Battery** estimates your energy reserves.
- **Fitbit's Stress Management Score** looks at how your body responds to stress.

Neither one measures cortisol or other stress hormones. Both use heart rate patterns to estimate something linked to stress.

Here's how each one works, what the science supports, and which might suit you better.

## How does Garmin Body Battery work?

**Body Battery isn't really a stress tracker. It's an estimate of your energy.**

It runs from 0 to 100. Higher numbers mean you have more in reserve for physical and mental demands. It goes up when you rest and down with activity and stress.

Garmin combines:

- heart rate variability (HRV), the change in time between heartbeats, during sleep and rest
- stress levels through the day
- sleep quality
- how hard you're active

When your HRV is high and steady, your Body Battery charges up. When you're active, stressed or short on sleep, it drains. It updates all day, so you get a live view of your energy.

The science behind it is solid in principle. HRV does reflect the balance of your nervous system, and lower HRV usually means more physical stress. Research shows HRV drops during mental and physical strain and recovers during rest.

## How does the Fitbit Stress Management Score work?

**Fitbit's Stress Management Score (SMS) is one daily score from 1 to 100. Higher is better.**

It doesn't track your energy moment to moment. Instead, it looks at how well your body handled stress over the past day.

The [Fitbit](/fitbit/) score looks at:

- **Responsiveness:** how your heart rate responds to physical activity
- **Exertion balance:** how many hard days you have compared with easy days
- **Sleep patterns:** how long and how well you sleep

A high score suggests your body is handling stress well. A low score suggests you might be pushing too hard without enough recovery. You get one score a day, not live updates like Body Battery.

Fitbit's approach is more about recovery patterns than stress right now. The responsiveness part is especially interesting. It checks whether your heart rate comes back to normal quickly after activity, which is a proven marker of heart fitness and stress resilience.

Neither one measures [stress](/stress/) hormones like cortisol. Both use indirect signs, mainly HRV and heart rate patterns. These are linked to stress, but they aren't the same as stress.

- **Body Battery is better for right now.** "Should I push hard in this workout?" or "Do I have the energy for this afternoon's meeting?"
- **Stress Management Score is better for the big picture.** Is your overall lifestyle balance working? It looks back over time.

## Which one is more accurate?

**That's the wrong question. Accuracy depends on what you're trying to measure.**

For deciding what to do today, Garmin's Body Battery gives more useful feedback. If it's low, you know you need to recover. Live updates show how each activity drains or restores you.

For checking whether your training, sleep and recovery habits work together, Fitbit's score gives a clearer big picture. The daily score helps you spot patterns over weeks.

Both have limits:

- HRV varies a lot from person to person, so comparing your score with someone else's is meaningless.
- Neither picks up mental stress that doesn't change your heart rate patterns.
- Caffeine, alcohol and illness can throw off readings.
- You need to wear the watch regularly for either one to work well.

The research on HRV as a stress marker is strong. But remember, these watches estimate HRV with light-based wrist sensors. Those aren't as accurate as chest straps or a medical ECG.

## Which should you pick?

If you're choosing between Garmin and Fitbit just for stress tracking, think about how you want to use the data.

- **Choose Garmin Body Battery** if you want moment-to-moment feedback on when to push and when to rest. It's especially useful for athletes timing hard efforts, or anyone planning their day around energy levels.
- **Choose Fitbit Stress Management Score** if you want to track long-term patterns and see whether lifestyle changes are lowering your stress load. It's better for spotting ongoing problems than for making decisions in the moment.
- **Or choose neither.** These numbers are interesting but not essential. Paying attention to your heart rate, your sleep and how you feel can get you most of the way there, without complicated formulas.
