## Does low HRV at night mean something is wrong?

**A low Apple Watch HRV reading overnight doesn't, on its own, mean your cortisol is high or that you recovered badly.**

Start by checking three things:

- what was measured
- when it was recorded
- how it compares with your own recent history

This guide is published by Cortisol+, an app that helps you explore patterns in your wearable data. It's not a diagnosis, and it can't turn HRV into hormone levels.

## What does my overnight HRV number mean?

Heart rate variability (HRV) is the small change in time between your heartbeats.

Apple's [technical paper on its heart measurements](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/Heart_Rate_Calorimetry_Activity_on_Apple_Watch_November_2024.pdf) uses SDNN, one way of summing up that change. Other wearables and apps may show a different measure, such as RMSSD.

Numbers from different methods, or from different stretches of time, **can't be swapped for each other**.

### A few samples aren't the whole night

An overnight reading can be useful. But a few samples don't describe every minute of your sleep.

Check the times on the samples before you treat a daily average as an overnight average.

Apple's [heart-rate guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120277) explains how recording depends on what you're doing and on your device's features. Follow the guidance for your watch and software version.

## Low HRV overnight on Apple Watch: a five-step check

1. **Find the source.** In Apple Health, open Heart Rate Variability. Look at the records and where they came from. Make sure they came from the device you expected.
2. **Check the times.** Were the samples really recorded while you were asleep? A daily summary can include awake readings too.
3. **Compare like with like.** Use the same device, the same HRV measure and similar conditions. Compare with your own history, not a friend's number.
4. **Add context.** Note when you slept, unusual exercise, alcohol, illness and how you felt. These notes help you ask better questions. They don't prove a cause.
5. **Look for a pattern that repeats.** One odd reading shouldn't change your plans or lead to a hormone diagnosis. If changes or symptoms keep going, talk to a healthcare professional.

If samples are **missing** rather than low, start with the [Apple Watch HRV troubleshooting guide](/blog/apple-watch-hrv-not-updating/). Missing data is not the same as an HRV of zero.

## Why is alcohol worth noting?

A [2026 study that observed wearable users](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0001284) linked drinking with lower HRV at night and a higher resting heart rate.

That doesn't mean everyone's HRV falls by a fixed amount. It also doesn't mean alcohol explains every low reading. Our [alcohol and HRV guide](/blog/alcohol-hrv-sleep/) explains the evidence and its limits.

## Is there a good overnight HRV number?

**This guide doesn't give one cutoff that splits a good night from a bad one.**

How it's measured, the conditions, and differences between people all matter. Start with our guide to [HRV ranges and how they're measured](/hrv/normal-range/), then look at your own similar records.

A higher number isn't always better. Don't change your breathing on purpose just to make a reading look better, and then compare it with a normal sleeping sample.

## Does low HRV during sleep mean high cortisol?

**No. HRV can't tell you how much cortisol is in your body.**

[Medical cortisol testing](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cortisol-test/) uses blood, saliva or urine. The timing is chosen to fit the medical question. A wearable score can't replace that, or rule out a hormone problem.

An earlier version of this article overstated the link between overnight HRV and cortisol. It also gave a fixed drop after one drink, which wasn't supported. We removed those claims. Read [what Apple Watch can and can't tell you about cortisol](/stress/apple-watch/).
