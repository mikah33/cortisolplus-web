**A low Apple Watch HRV reading overnight does not, by itself, mean that your cortisol is high or that you recovered poorly.** Start by checking what was measured, when it was recorded and how it compares with your own recent history.

This guide is published by Cortisol+, an app that helps you explore wearable patterns. It is not a diagnosis or a way to convert HRV into hormone levels.

## What your overnight HRV number represents

HRV describes variation in the time between heartbeats. Apple's [technical explanation of its heart measurements](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/Heart_Rate_Calorimetry_Activity_on_Apple_Watch_November_2024.pdf) documents SDNN, one method of summarizing that variation. Other wearables and apps may display a different metric, such as RMSSD. Values from different methods or recording windows are not interchangeable.

An overnight reading can be useful context, but a few samples do not describe every minute of sleep. Check the underlying timestamps before treating a daily average as an overnight average. Apple's [heart-rate documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120277) explains how recording depends on activity and device features; follow the guidance for your Watch and software version.

## Apple Watch HRV low overnight: a five-step check

1. **Find the source.** In Apple Health, open Heart Rate Variability and examine the available records and data sources. Confirm that the values came from the device you expected.
2. **Check the timestamps.** Were the samples actually recorded while you were asleep? A daily summary can include awake measurements too.
3. **Compare like with like.** Use the same device, HRV metric and approximate recording conditions. Compare with your own history rather than a friend's number.
4. **Add context.** Note sleep timing, unusual exercise, alcohol, illness and how you felt. These observations can help you ask better questions; they do not establish a cause.
5. **Look for a repeated pattern.** A single outlier should not determine your plans or prompt a hormone diagnosis. Persistent changes or symptoms deserve discussion with a healthcare professional.

If samples are absent rather than low, use the [Apple Watch HRV troubleshooting guide](/blog/apple-watch-hrv-not-updating/) first. Missing data is not a zero HRV reading.

## Why alcohol is worth noting

A [2026 observational study of wearable users](https://journals.plos.org/digitalhealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pdig.0001284) associated alcohol intake with lower nocturnal HRV and higher resting heart rate. This does not mean every person's HRV falls by a fixed number, or that alcohol explains every low reading. Our [alcohol and HRV guide](/blog/alcohol-hrv-sleep/) explains the evidence and its limits.

## Is there a good overnight HRV number?

There is no single cutoff in this guide that separates a good night from a bad one. Measurement method, recording conditions and individual differences matter. Start with our explanation of [HRV ranges and measurement methods](/hrv/normal-range/), then review your own comparable records.

A higher number is not automatically better in every situation. Avoid deliberately changing your breathing just to make a reading look better and then comparing it with a spontaneous sleeping sample.

## Does low sleeping HRV mean high cortisol?

**No. HRV cannot establish your cortisol concentration.** [Clinical cortisol testing](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/cortisol-test/) uses blood, saliva or urine, with timing chosen for the clinical question. A wearable score cannot replace that process or rule out a hormone disorder.

Our earlier article overstated the relationship between overnight HRV and cortisol and gave an unsupported fixed drop after one drink. Those claims have been removed. Read [what Apple Watch can and cannot tell you about cortisol](/stress/apple-watch/).
