**A high stress score on your watch is not a blood pressure reading.**

Feeling stressed also doesn't prove that cortisol is causing high blood pressure. These are different measurements, used for different things by doctors.

## Where does cortisol fit in?

**Cortisol is only one possible piece.**

In Cushing's syndrome, the body has too much cortisol for a long time. This can be linked with high blood pressure. But that doesn't mean every high blood pressure reading has a cortisol cause. [NIDDK describes the condition and its complications](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome).

High blood pressure has many risk factors and possible causes, [NHLBI explains](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/high-blood-pressure/causes). These include:

- Age
- Family history
- Health conditions
- Medicines

Stress can play a part. But an app can't tell these causes apart, or decide which treatment is right.

## Why keep the real readings separate?

**Only a blood pressure reading tells you your blood pressure.**

If your clinician asks you to check your blood pressure at home, use the device and method they recommend. Keep the readings, dates and any useful notes together.

Don't use a heart rate reading, HRV (heart rate variability) value or Cortisol+ score in place of a blood pressure result.

Good questions to ask at an appointment:

- Were my readings taken with the right equipment and technique?
- Could any medicines or health conditions I have matter here?
- What follow-up readings do you want, and when should I contact you?

## What does Cortisol+ add?

**Cortisol+ helps you look at your watch signals next to your own notes.** That's all.

- It does not diagnose high blood pressure (hypertension).
- It does not measure blood pressure.
- It can't show that your cortisol changed.

**A lower app score is not a reason to change prescribed treatment.**
