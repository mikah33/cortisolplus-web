A high wearable stress score is not a blood pressure reading. Feeling stressed does not establish that cortisol is causing high blood pressure, either. These are different measurements with different clinical uses.

## Where cortisol fits

Long-term cortisol excess in Cushing's syndrome can be associated with high blood pressure. That does not mean every elevated blood pressure result has a cortisol cause. [NIDDK describes the condition and its complications](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/cushings-syndrome).

[NHLBI explains that high blood pressure has multiple risk factors and possible causes](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/high-blood-pressure/causes), including age, family history, health conditions and medicines. Stress can contribute, but an app cannot distinguish those possibilities or decide which treatment is appropriate.

## Keep the actual measurements separate

If your clinician asks you to monitor blood pressure at home, use the device and measurement procedure they recommend. Keep the readings, dates and relevant notes together. Do not substitute a heart rate reading, HRV value or Cortisol+ score for a blood pressure result.

Useful questions for an appointment include:

- Were my readings taken with suitable equipment and technique?
- Could any medicines or existing conditions be relevant?
- What follow-up measurements do you want, and when should I contact you?

## What Cortisol+ contributes

You can use the app to review available wearable signals alongside your own notes. It does not diagnose hypertension, measure blood pressure or establish that cortisol changed. A lower app score is not a reason to change prescribed treatment. Our [methodology](/how-it-works/) explains the difference between the wellness model and clinical measurements.
