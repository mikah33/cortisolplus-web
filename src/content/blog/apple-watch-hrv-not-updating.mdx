Heart rate variability (HRV) is the small change in time between your heartbeats. Apple Watch saves it to the Health app on your iPhone.

If your HRV isn't updating, **first check whether the newest reading is missing from Apple Health, or only from the app showing it.** That tells you if it's a recording or syncing problem, or an app-access problem.

A missing reading on its own does not tell you your stress or cortisol level.

## 1. Is the newest HRV reading in Apple Health?

On your iPhone:

1. Open **Health** and tap **Search**.
2. Search for **Heart Rate Variability** and open it.
3. Under **Options**, tap **Show All Data** to see each reading.
4. Check the date and time of the **newest** reading, not the daily average.
5. Under **Data Sources & Access**, see which apps or devices add data.

These labels follow Apple's iOS 26 guide. The menus can be different on other versions. See [Apple's guide to viewing Health data](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/view-your-health-data-iphe3d379c32/26/ios/26).

What did you find?

- **Health has recent data, but your wellness app is older:** check the app's permissions and the time it shows (step 2).
- **Health has no recent data:** check watch fit and power settings (steps 3 and 4). Also notice if other watch readings are missing too.

## 2. Can the app read your HRV?

In Health, tap your profile picture. Under **Privacy**, tap **Apps**. Choose Cortisol+ or the app you use and check its access to Heart Rate Variability, if it's listed.

Only turn on the data you want to share. Apple explains these controls in [Manage Health data](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108779).

**Permission only lets an app read readings that already exist.** Changing it doesn't make a new HRV reading.

Reopen the app and compare its time with the reading you found in Health. To see what goes into our wellness score, and its limits, read [how Cortisol+ works](/how-it-works/).

## 3. Is the watch fitting well?

The back of the watch needs to touch your skin.

- Make the band feel secure but comfortable.
- Wear the watch above your wrist bone, a little toward your elbow.
- Don't tighten it until it hurts.

Apple's [watch fitting guidance](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118234) explains where to wear it and why the sensor needs contact.

## 4. Is Low Power Mode on?

On the watch, go to **Settings > Battery > Low Power Mode**.

Apple says this mode **turns off background heart-rate readings** and cuts back background app refresh. If it's on, think about whether you need the battery savings while you look into the missing data.

Apple's [Low Power Mode documentation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108320) doesn't promise how often HRV will update.

## How often should Apple Watch HRV update?

**Apple doesn't give a fixed timetable for HRV.**

Apple's heart-rate page says background **heart-rate** readings happen at times that change with your activity. It also says an optical sensor is used for HRV. See [Apple's heart-rate monitoring explanation](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120277).

So a fresh heart-rate reading doesn't prove there's a fresh HRV reading. Check the HRV time itself.

## What if HRV still isn't updating?

Before you contact support, write down:

- the time of your last HRV reading
- your watch model
- your iOS and watchOS versions
- whether the reading shows up in Health

This helps you describe the problem without guessing the cause.

Where to go next:

- **Readings missing from Health:** start with [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/contact).
- **Health has recent readings, but your app shows old ones:** contact that app's support and share what you compared.

A stale wellness score is not a current hormone measurement. Our [Apple Watch stress and cortisol guide](/stress/apple-watch/) explains what the watch can and can't tell you.
