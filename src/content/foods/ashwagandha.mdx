## Does ashwagandha lower cortisol?

**Some trials report lower cortisol in the blood of stressed adults taking specific ashwagandha extracts.**

That's narrower than saying every ashwagandha product lowers cortisol safely for everyone. Who was studied, which preparation they took and how long they were followed all matter.

## A closer look at one trial

[Salve and colleagues' 2019 randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32021735/) enrolled 60 stressed adults.

- They took 250 mg/day or 600 mg/day of the studied root extract, or a placebo (dummy pill), for eight weeks.
- 58 people finished the study.
- The authors reported less perceived stress and lower blood (serum) cortisol in the extract groups.

**Those amounts describe the study. They are not a dosing recommendation for you.**

A small, short trial of one preparation can't show:

- long-term safety
- that other brands work the same way
- that it's a reliable treatment for a hormone disorder

A group result that is statistically significant also can't tell you how much *your own* cortisol would change.

## How long does ashwagandha take to lower cortisol?

**There's no proven timeline.**

The trial above followed people for eight weeks. That's not a promise a supplement will work within eight weeks, and it doesn't show an immediate effect.

Cortisol+ can't check a hormone change either. Its wearable-based wellness score measures something different.

## What remains uncertain

**Different extracts contain different ingredients.** A product labelled "ashwagandha" isn't necessarily the same as the one that was tested.

Quoting the biggest percentage from a study also hides who was studied, what they were compared with and how uncertain the result was.

## Is ashwagandha safe?

**Check with a clinician or pharmacist before you take it.** [NCCIH's ashwagandha guidance](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha) notes:

- There's limited evidence on long-term safety.
- It may cause stomach upset and drowsiness.
- There are rare reports of liver injury.

**Who should avoid it or take extra care:**

- Avoid it during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- It isn't recommended around surgery, or for people with thyroid or autoimmune disorders.
- People with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer should avoid it.

It can interact with thyroid, blood-pressure, diabetes, sedative, anticonvulsant (anti-seizure) and immunosuppressant medicines.

## Can a wearable show whether it worked?

**No.** A change in heart rate variability (HRV), sleep or a wellness score doesn't show a change in cortisol.

Many things can change at the same time. A before-and-after chart can't single out what a supplement did.
