## What is "cortisol face"?

**"Cortisol face" is a social media name for a puffy, rounded face blamed on high cortisol.**

When it's medically real, doctors call it **"moon face"**. It's a well-known sign of Cushing's syndrome.

## The honest reality

**Most people calling out "cortisol face" online don't have Cushing's.**

Facial puffiness has many more common causes:

- salt (sodium) in your food
- alcohol
- poor sleep
- allergies
- your natural face shape and body build

That said, **very high cortisol over a long time, as in Cushing's syndrome, can change the face**. It can do this through:

- **Fluid retention:** high cortisol makes the body hold on to more sodium and water.
- **Fat shifting:** fat moves toward the face and trunk.
- **Thinner skin:** long-term high cortisol breaks down collagen, the protein that keeps skin firm.

Moon face appears when cortisol stays far above normal **for months or years**. The usual causes are steroid medicines taken for a long time, a tumor, or another hormone disorder.

Cushing's syndrome is rare. A single puffy morning is not moon face.

## What the TikTok trend gets wrong

**The viral version makes a rare medical condition sound common.** Two mistakes show up again and again:

- **Water retention is not fat shifting.** Stress can wreck your sleep and leave you puffy in the morning. But that's fluid, swelling and camera angles, not the fat changes of moon face.
- **Faces change for many reasons.** Weight gain, aging, genes, thyroid problems, allergies, alcohol and salt all change how your face looks. Blaming cortisol alone ignores the more likely causes.

## How to know if your "cortisol face" is real

A few questions help separate everyday puffiness from true cortisol excess:

- **Timing:** salt-driven puffiness comes and goes overnight. Face changes from high cortisol last for weeks or months.
- **Other signs:** real cortisol excess rarely affects only the face. Look for a fatty pad on the upper back ("buffalo hump"), wide purple stretch marks, thin skin that bruises easily, weight gain around the middle with thinner arms and legs, and muscle weakness. A puffy face on its own is unlikely to be Cushing's.
- **What a wearable can't tell you:** apps like Cortisol+ show trends in sleep, HRV and resting heart rate. **They don't measure cortisol**, so they can't say whether cortisol is behind your face changes.
- **When to see a doctor:** if face changes have lasted for a while, or come with any of the other signs above, see a doctor. They can decide whether [cortisol testing](/cortisol/test/) makes sense, and which test to use.

## What to do about it

**If you have signs of Cushing's, that needs medical care, not just new habits.**

For everyday puffiness and stress, these are sensible places to start:

- **Sleep first.** Short or poor sleep is a common cause of a puffy morning face. See [sleep and cortisol](/cortisol/lower/sleep/).
- **Cut back on alcohol.** Alcohol is one of the common causes of facial puffiness.
- **Slow breathing.** A few minutes of slow, relaxed breathing can help you wind down. See [breathing exercises for stress](/cortisol/lower/breathing-exercises/).
- **Change one thing at a time.** Then you can tell what actually helped. Keep notes on sleep, alcohol, salty meals and how your face looks.

See [the full how-to-lower guide](/cortisol/lower/) for what the evidence says, and its limits.
